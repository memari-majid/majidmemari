Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

Value creation and value capture are different

A solution creates value when it helps someone achieve a useful outcome. A commercial offering captures some of that value through a price or revenue model. An internal business project may instead justify its cost through better service, avoided losses, or increased capacity. State whether your capstone is an internal improvement or an external product/service so its economic logic is clear. An offer must also specify what the buyer receives, how much support or revision is included, and what happens next. Treat a proposed price or resource request as a hypothesis until supported. A classroom offer card is not a binding agreement or evidence of a sale.

Use a business model as a connected argument

Describe the customer, problem, value proposition, delivery approach, costs, benefits or revenue, and success measures. These elements should agree. A low-price service that requires extensive personal review may be difficult to sustain. A premium service needs a defensible reason customers would pay more. A one-page business-model canvas is useful when its claims are specific and its assumptions are visible.

Differentiate through customer understanding and delivery

Using the same AI tool as competitors is not, by itself, a durable advantage. A business may distinguish itself through a focused customer need, trusted relationships, useful packaging, reliable review, convenience, or a better service experience. Explain how the offer fits into the customer’s existing work: where they find it, what they supply, what they receive, and why switching is worthwhile. The user may not be the buyer, so identify who approves payment or staff effort. Compare existing alternatives, including templates and doing nothing. A promising concept still needs a plausible route to customers and a way to deliver consistent value.

Worked pricing comparison: delivery effort changes the model

Consider explicitly hypothetical figures for a reviewed listing service: eight minutes of staff work at $24 per hour costs $3.20 per listing. Add an assumed $0.80 allowance for other variable delivery costs, giving $4 per listing before fixed costs and customer acquisition. At $8 per listing, contribution is $4 per listing; that is not net profit. A $99 subscription including 25 listings produces only $3.96 per listing at full use, slightly below the assumed $4 variable cost. At ten listings of use, it leaves $59 before fixed and acquisition costs. This difference shows why usage, review effort, and support expectations matter. Do not choose a price solely because it sounds affordable. Explain who pays, what they receive, what work delivery requires, and what assumptions would make the offer sustainable.

Fictional business case

Put the ideas to work

ListingLift could be sold as a monthly subscription, a per-listing service, or an internal tool used by a retailer’s staff. The concept still requires a person to check every product fact. The founder has not decided whether customers value speed, consistency, or hands-on review most. The user and buyer may differ: an employee prepares listings while an owner approves spending. The current alternative is a reusable template plus staff review. Any proposed price, usage volume, or customer commitment must be labeled as an assumption unless evidence is actually supplied.

Discuss the decision

  1. How would the three models change the customer relationship and cost structure?
  2. What might customers pay for beyond the generated text?
  3. Which assumption should be tested before choosing a model?

Business numbers · interpret the evidence

Subscription usage changes contribution

Fictional teaching data, not real customer, vendor, or pilot observations. Hypothetical ListingLift subscription: $99 monthly revenue and $4 variable delivery cost per listing. Variable cost includes an assumed $3.20 staff-time valuation plus $0.80 other delivery costs. Fixed costs, acquisition, and taxes are excluded.

On a narrow screen, scroll the table sideways. Keyboard: focus the table and use the arrow keys.

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Why does the same subscription price produce different contribution?
  2. At full use of 25 listings, what are revenue and variable cost per listing?
  3. What usage threshold should be investigated before promising 25 included listings?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

At 25 listings, revenue per listing is $99 ÷ 25 = $3.96, below the assumed $4 variable cost. Contribution is $99 − $100 = −$1 before fixed or acquisition costs. At ten listings, contribution is $59 because fewer units consume delivery effort. The mathematical zero-contribution point is $99 ÷ $4 = 24.75 listings, so 24 whole listings remain above zero and 25 below it under the assumptions. This is contribution, not net profit or necessarily cash margin when staff time is valued rather than newly paid. A sustainable offer must account for usage variation and excluded costs.

Workshop · create an artifact

Draft the business model

  1. 1State whether your capstone is an internal initiative or an external offering.
  2. 2Complete a one-page model covering user, buyer or budget owner, problem, value, delivery, alternatives, economics, and measures. For an internal project, explain who authorizes the effort even when there is no sales price.
  3. 3Compare two ways to deliver, price, or fund the offer. State what is included, how customers or internal users find it, and how usage, human review, and support affect cost.
  4. 4Identify three assumptions that materially affect viability.
  5. 5Prepare questions for next week’s concept validation exercise.
  6. 6Use this module’s data table to calculate and interpret the requested quantities. Keep counts, denominators, units, and uncertainty visible; incorporate the relevant table or small figure into the existing artifact rather than creating another submission.

Discussion · make your reasoning visible

Why Would This Business Model Work?

Explain how your concept creates value and how that value supports a price or internal investment. Identify an existing alternative and one reason a customer or manager might choose your proposal instead. Write 150–250 words and respond to one classmate in 75–100 words with a specific question or improvement. Distinguish case facts, assumptions, and AI-generated suggestions; disclose any AI assistance. Use fictional or supplied case data. Use the instructor’s calendar for your live offering.

Suggested semester scoring: 5 points, with 3 for the original contribution and 2 for a constructive reply. Follow the arrangements designated for your offering. Independent learners can write a response and compare it with a peer.

Knowledge check · ungraded

Check your reasoning

A listing service has $4 variable cost per listing. A $99 plan includes 25 listings. At full use, what remains before fixed and acquisition costs?
25 × $4 = $100 in variable delivery cost, so $99 − $100 = −$1 before fixed costs and acquisition. At lower usage the result differs. Pricing must account for workload and review effort, not just recurring revenue.
