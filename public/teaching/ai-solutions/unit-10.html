Unit 10 of 12

Bring the solution into use

How will people begin, complete a useful task, and give actionable feedback?

Learn

By the end of this unit

  • Plan a bounded rollout, onboarding, support, and a proportionate next decision.
  • Distinguish reach, conversation, agreement, and observed successful use with named denominators.

Planned guided work including textbook reading: 85–135 minutes, plus project work. This is a design estimate.

Support the first real task

A useful solution still needs an appropriate route to the people it is intended to help. For ReplyKit, invite a coordinator to an approved classroom or limited pilot task. For a personal tool, arrange an appropriate reviewer attempt; for a community or research project, use the agreed contact and information boundary. You do not need an advertising campaign, a sale, or public deployment to demonstrate supported use.

Tell the user what the current version does, what it does not do, how to access it, which input to try, how a person checks the result, and where to get help. Ask the responsible owner before changing a real process or using other people's information. A role-play can help rehearse this explanation; record the separate observed task when someone actually tries the solution.

Fictional supported-adoption example; one cohort and one observation period
StageObserved count in this supplied exampleWhat the count establishes
Reached100 people receive an appropriate invitation.The invitation reached them; this is not evidence of use.
Conversation20 discuss the task and access requirements.20/100 = 20% of those reached; needs and questions may be clarified.
Pilot agreement5 agree to attempt the bounded pilot.5/20 = 25% of conversations; 5/100 = 5% of those reached. Agreement is not completion.
Successful first task3 actually complete the defined task within the period and meet its quality check.3/5 = 60% of agreements; 3/100 = 3% of those reached. Record assistance and failures as well.

Define each stage and observation period before counting. In this example, successful first-task completion requires the user to produce an acceptable checked output or handoff using the released version. A click or enthusiastic comment does not meet that definition. The two agreeing users without observed completion need honest status labels; their reasons are unknown unless investigated.

Suppose preparation and onboarding for this cohort use $150 cash and five hours valued at $24/hour: $270 resource cost including valued time. Divide by the three observed successful first-task completions: $90 per successful completion. Dividing by five yields $54 per agreement; dividing by twenty yields $13.50 per conversation. These are different metrics, with the same stated cost boundary. None is customer acquisition cost, cash cost alone, or a forecast for a larger rollout.

Rehearse onboarding, then investigate a blockage

Use a classmate or the supplied coordinator role. Explain the intended task and limits, show the start location, invite a permitted input, and check understanding. Ask, “What would stop you from trying this?” A possible refusal is useful evidence, not a failed sale.

Original worked onboarding conversation

Coordinator: “I can open the file, but I do not know which policy is current.” Learner: “Use source-card version 1 from this Start page. If that approved source is unavailable or has changed, hold the draft and ask the owner.”

Coordinator: “Does this send the reply or change the booking?” Learner: “No. It prepares a draft or exception note. The named reviewer checks the source and controls release; the coordinator must still confirm any actual booking action.”

Next step: Ask the coordinator to locate S3 and attempt the 60-hour case. Record whether the attempt actually occurred, the output, assistance, and one instruction to improve. A rehearsed conversation alone does not establish successful use.

Choose one and write why before revealing the evidence.

Reveal evidence and challenge
  • Funnel: 20 invited → 8 agreed → 3 observed first-task completions.
  • Resource calculation: $180/3 = $60 per observed successful first task, not $22.50 per agreement.
  • Challenge: Two completers needed direct help; continued independent use has not been observed.

What would you change before expanding outreach? Distinguish first-task success, assistance, and continued use.

Compare with the worked reasoning

The observed outcome costs $60 in resource value per successful first task in this tiny fictional trial. Keep the two assisted completions visible. Improve onboarding and test independent use and support effort before increasing invitations. Three first tasks do not establish retention, paid demand, profitability, or a generalizable conversion rate.

Apply to your project: Define one observable adoption step, its denominator, the assistance you will record, and the next follow-up.
