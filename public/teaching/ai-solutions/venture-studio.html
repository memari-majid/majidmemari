Venture studio

From a real problem to a first customer.

Discover demand, build a usable AI-assisted solution, find customers, sell a bounded offer, and deliver reliably. Use the same journey for a new business or a service inside an organization.

From demand to delivery

Build the whole business process.

Use these stages for a small venture or a new service inside an organization. A one-person AI-assisted business is a scenario to evaluate, not a prediction or a guarantee of demand. A private classroom pilot and supplied fictional evidence are valid routes.

01

Discover a worthwhile need

Identify a specific person, recurring problem, current alternative, and consequence. For an external venture, distinguish the user from the person who can approve spending. Use a real public business context where appropriate or the complete fictional case packets.

Evidence
A source-based problem description, neutral discovery questions, and accurately labeled observations, role-play, or supplied responses. An AI-generated persona is a hypothesis, not a customer.
Usable artifact
A one-page problem brief and evidence/assumption log.
AI assistance
Help organize public or fictional information, propose questions, and summarize supplied responses without inventing interviews.
Human check
Verify material facts, avoid leading questions, and decide whether the problem matters enough to pursue.
Measure
Problem frequency, time or consequence, and evidence confidence; show the count and denominator.

02

Shape an offer people can understand

Turn the need into a clear offer: who it helps, what useful result is delivered, what is included, and what remains the customer’s responsibility. Compare a simpler non-AI alternative.

Evidence
A concrete example of the promised output and feedback on whether the intended user understands its purpose and limits.
Usable artifact
A short offer card and a finished sample, document kit, service storyboard, or testable slide prototype.
AI assistance
Generate alternative value propositions, draft sample content, and help compare scope and delivery options.
Human check
Check claims and distinguish an illustrative concept from an implemented or validated product.
Measure
Correct understanding of the offer, unresolved questions, and the cost of delivering the promised scope.

03

Implement and deploy a small usable solution

Construct the minimum useful product or service with existing no-code tools. Check it against three supplied cases, then make a versioned artifact available to an approved partner or classroom test user and record an actual task and revision. A prototype must connect to a usable manual output or service; a mockup alone is insufficient.

Evidence
The artifact, a small pass/fail or quality table, the observed classroom test process, and a record of what changed. Classroom testing is not market validation.
Usable artifact
Implemented solution, controlled deployment/access record, user-test evidence, and a concise revision log.
AI assistance
Assist with drafting, organizing, checking, and revising specific outputs. A role-based AI assistant can perform a delegated task without requiring an autonomous agent system.
Human check
Specify inputs, approved sources, acceptance checks, boundaries, and escalation. A person approves the final deliverable.
Measure
Factual quality, task completion, total preparation/review time, and unresolved failures.

04

Price the offer and check viability

For a venture, choose a price and delivery model that fits the customer and full cost of service. For an internal initiative, identify the budget owner, expected value, and funding rationale. Compare a less favorable scenario.

Evidence
Visible assumptions for price, usage, staff effort, acquisition, recurring expense, and capacity. Do not confuse an assigned value for time with realized cash.
Usable artifact
A small economics table, scenario chart, and decision threshold.
AI assistance
Help list cost categories, explain calculations, and explore clearly labeled alternatives.
Human check
Verify arithmetic and units, challenge missing costs, and decide whether the proposed scope is sustainable.
Measure
Contribution per order, acquisition cost per new customer, recurring value, and a clearly defined break-even or stop threshold.

05

Find prospective customers or internal users

Choose one plausible channel and a specific audience. Prepare a useful message and call to action. Plan how people move from awareness to qualified interest. An internal project needs an adoption route just as a venture needs acquisition.

Evidence
A documented channel rationale and supplied or appropriately collected counts for reach, qualified inquiries, and next steps. Different audiences and small samples limit causal claims.
Usable artifact
A small acquisition or adoption kit: message, channel, audience, call to action, and funnel table.
AI assistance
Draft message variants, tailor language to public or fictional audience needs, and summarize campaign counts.
Human check
Review truthfulness, permission and audience fit, and approve any real outreach. Sending messages or publishing a campaign is not required.
Measure
Qualified-inquiry rate, cost per qualified inquiry, and conversion to a defined next step; always identify denominators.

06

Sell or secure internal approval

Use a concise conversation to understand fit, present the offer, address an objection, and agree on a next step. A venture may propose a purchase; an internal initiative may seek permission for a bounded pilot. Role-play is sufficient.

Evidence
A realistic objection, a supported response, and a clearly labeled record of the role-play or actual interaction. Interest, intent, and a completed purchase are different evidence.
Usable artifact
An offer/proposal card and short sales or approval conversation script with scope, price or resource request, and next step.
AI assistance
Rehearse objections, suggest clarifying questions, and draft a proposal using approved facts.
Human check
Do not invent commitments, guarantees, testimonials, or purchases. A person negotiates terms and authorizes any real agreement.
Measure
Qualified prospect-to-proposal and proposal-to-order rates, or internal pilot approval; no actual sale or payment is required.

07

Deliver, support, and retain

Define what happens after acceptance: intake, preparation, review, delivery, support, and improvement. Make the handoff understandable. For a recurring service, explain why customers would return and how you would learn from problems.

Evidence
A tested delivery checklist, sample output, escalation rule, and clearly labeled quality or service-time record.
Usable artifact
Deployed product or service, operating instructions, review/escalation checklist, and user handoff.
AI assistance
Support bounded roles such as research assistant, draft preparer, quality-check helper, and service-summary assistant. Separate proposed delegation from actions actually performed.
Human check
Retain responsibility for final quality, promises, exceptions, and customer information. Use manual handoffs if no automated tools are involved.
Measure
On-time delivery, first-pass quality, rework, customer effort, and repeat purchase or continued use within a defined eligible cohort.

08

Improve, decide what to scale, and remain accountable

Review the evidence and choose to improve, narrow, stop, or expand. Consider a small operator coordinating several AI-assisted tasks as a possible operating model, not a guaranteed future or a promise of effortless profit.

Evidence
Actual controlled classroom or partner test observations, clearly separated from supplied scenario figures, plus the limits of the agreed pilot and next-step decision.
Usable artifact
A decision memo and next-step operating plan, supported by the product/service artifact and a clear evidence trail.
AI assistance
Summarize operating information, generate improvement options, and help update scenarios while keeping assumptions visible.
Human check
Approve expansion, check capacity and dependencies, and prevent delegated tasks from making unsupported commitments.
Measure
Contribution, retention or continued use, quality, workload, and the threshold that would change the recommendation.

A complete worked example · all facts and numbers are fictional

A one-person listing-review service

Cedar Copy Studio helps small retailers turn approved product facts into consistent, reviewed listing packs. AI assists with drafting and checking structure; the owner remains accountable for the final work.

This is a classroom planning case, not an operating business or a claim about future earnings. A one-person business supported by AI is one possible operating model. Whether it works depends on demand, quality, cost, and the owner’s capacity.

01

Find a specific problem

Customer: independent retailers with small, frequently changing product catalogs. Problem hypothesis: preparing consistent listings consumes staff time, and hurried copy can omit important facts.

Fictional discovery packet: useful clues, not validated market demand
Supplied observationWhat it supportsWhat remains unknown
One manager reports spending 90 minutes on a batch of five listings.A possible workflow problem worth exploring.Whether the workload recurs and whether outsourcing would help.
A second manager says consistency matters more than speed.Quality criteria belong in the offer.What “consistent” means and what errors are costly.
A third manager asks to see a sample before discussing price.A concrete sample could improve the conversation.Willingness to pay, repeat demand, and purchase authority.

Next discovery question: “Walk me through the last time you prepared a new listing. Where did time or uncertainty arise?” Ask for the current process before pitching a solution.

02

Make a bounded offer

For independent retailers preparing small product batches, Cedar Copy Studio delivers five reviewed listing drafts from your approved product facts, plus a source checklist and one revision round. Planning price: $79 per batch. Delivery target: three business days after complete inputs and scope approval.

Included: five titles and descriptions, missing-fact flags, and an editable handoff file. Excluded: product photography, store administration, publishing, guarantees of sales, and claims the customer cannot document.

Simpler alternative: a reusable listing template completed by the retailer. The service must offer enough benefit over that option to justify its price.

03

Build a sample someone can judge

SAMPLE DELIVERABLE / ONE OF FIVE LISTINGS

Approved input facts

12-ounce ceramic mug · navy glaze · dishwasher safe · supplied retail price $14. Microwave suitability, country of manufacture, shipping, and warranty are not supplied.

Reviewed listing

Navy ceramic mug, 12 oz — $14
A 12-ounce ceramic mug with a navy glaze. Dishwasher safe. Contact the retailer to confirm any care details not listed here.

Review record

  • Capacity, material, color, care claim, and price match the supplied facts.
  • Removed the AI draft’s unsupported “handmade” and “microwave-safe” claims.
  • Flagged missing shipping and warranty information for the retailer.
  • Owner approves the draft; the retailer controls publication.

Acceptance test: every factual claim traces to an approved input, all required fields are present, and unsupported facts remain clearly flagged. Ask a reviewer to find care information and explain what still requires confirmation.

04

Price the complete work

Illustrative economics for one five-listing batch. Owner time is valued at an assumed $24/hour.

These are planning assumptions, not current vendor prices. If review takes an extra 30 minutes, contribution falls by $12 to $32. The price must cover the complete service, including corrections and communication.

For a month with three orders, contribution is 3 × $44 = $132. Subtract $60 of separate fixed overhead and five acquisition hours valued at $24/hour: $132 − $60 − $120 = −$48 of illustrative economic surplus. This excludes taxes and other unspecified costs. It is not a cash-profit calculation: owner time is an economic cost even when no wage is paid. A promising unit contribution does not establish a viable business.

05

Find customers through a testable channel

Start with one relevant channel, such as an opt-in local retailer workshop where an owner can examine the sample and request a conversation. AI can help draft educational material and organize a prospect hypothesis; it does not create permission to contact people or send messages.

Interpret the forecast: response rate 12/40 = 30%; conversation rate among responses 6/12 = 50%; purchase rate among conversations 3/6 = 50%; overall purchase rate 3/40 = 7.5%. Small changes compound across the funnel. These assumed rates must be tested and cannot be described as proven demand.

Measure: counts at each stage, source of each inquiry, reasons for declining, acquisition time, and customer acquisition cash cost. Do not count a compliment, email reply, or generated persona as a purchase.

06

Sell honestly and learn from the answer

“How are you preparing new product listings today? If the review work is a recurring problem, I can show a sample of a five-listing pack built from approved facts. The proposed pilot is $79, includes one revision, and leaves publication with you. Would reviewing the sample help you decide whether this solves a problem worth paying for?”

If the customer asks for guaranteed sales: “I can commit to the agreed deliverable and review process. I cannot guarantee sales. We can define how you will evaluate the pack’s accuracy and usefulness.”

If the customer says it is too expensive: ask which part of the work matters and compare the full service with the reusable-template alternative. Avoid an unsupported claim about savings. Record the objection as evidence to investigate, not a reason to invent urgency or a testimonial.

Classroom route: role-play the conversation and mark every assumption. Deliver the usable pack to a classroom reviewer, observe its use, and practice the sales or adoption pitch. Real outreach, a public launch, payment collection, and customer commitments are optional.

07

Onboard and deliver with a clear AI-assisted process

  1. Scope: the owner confirms the five products, deliverables, price, revision boundary, and timing.
  2. Inputs: the customer supplies approved product facts and confirms permission to use the material. Missing facts stay missing until verified.
  3. Draft: an AI drafting role produces structured copy from the approved facts only.
  4. Review: an AI checking role flags unsupported claims; the owner independently checks the facts and arithmetic.
  5. Delivery: the owner approves the editable pack and the handoff note. The customer decides what to publish.
  6. Follow-up: ask what required revision, whether the pack was used, and whether another batch would be useful.

Track on-time accepted batches, rework minutes, important errors, complaints, and repeat orders. A second purchase or repeated use tells you something different from initial interest. A tiny repeat-customer sample still cannot support a broad retention claim.

08

Delegate tasks without delegating accountability

A ready-made AI assistant can play each role in a separate, manually reviewed step. An automated agent system is optional. Begin with the simplest process that provides reliable evidence and a clear stop point.

Proposed AI-assisted team: bounded roles, with a person approving every external action
RolePermitted classroom taskHuman gate

Stop conditions: unresolved product claims, missing permission, repeated material errors, or insufficient capacity to meet the promise. A manual fallback is part of the design. More automation is justified by demonstrated value and reliable controls, not by the number of agents.

Inspect the model and fixed assumptions

Monthly acquisition spending is $300; software is $60; direct cash delivery cost is $12 per fulfilled order. Owner time is valued at $30/hour and is shown separately from cash expenses. No payroll payment to the owner is included. Taxes, setup costs, refunds, payment fees, and collection delays are excluded from this simplified exercise; include relevant items in a real pilot.

Each order represents one new customer buying once in this scenario. Enquiries = visits × enquiry rate. Orders = enquiries × close rate. Both are rounded down to whole people. Capacity = available hours × 60 ÷ delivery minutes, rounded down. Fulfilled orders = the smaller of orders and capacity. Unfilled orders are declined or waitlisted: no payment or revenue is assumed for them. Revenue is collected for completed deliveries in this scenario.

Cash operating remainder = collected revenue − direct delivery cost − acquisition spending − software. Contribution after valuing owner time subtracts fulfilled orders × delivery minutes ÷ 60 × $30. Acquisition spend per fulfilled customer uses fulfilled orders as its denominator; it is not calculated when there are no deliveries. This is one-month scenario accounting, not a complete profit or cash-flow forecast.

Displayed values are rounded; calculations use unrounded time. Changing price does not automatically change demand in this model. Reducing review time does not prove that quality is maintained. Test both assumptions with evidence before relying on the apparent gain. Rates are invented scenario assumptions; real demand and timing vary.

Make the operating decision

  1. Predict whether demand or delivery capacity is the bottleneck.
  2. Change one assumption. Explain the effect on fulfilled orders and the two cost measures.
  3. Choose one test: demand at the proposed price, reliable delivery time, or acceptable output quality.
  4. Record the scenario and decision in your launch plan. Name a result that would make you pause.
