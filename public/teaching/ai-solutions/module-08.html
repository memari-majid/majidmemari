What you will be able to do

  • Identify material business risks and assign responsibility for handling them.
  • Compare solution options using business needs and verifiable vendor information.
  • Explain what should remain under human review in the chosen concept.

Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

Responsibility belongs in the business design

Consider who could be affected by inaccurate content, unfair treatment, disclosure of information, or an inappropriate promise. A risk statement should name the possible failure, who is affected, and its consequence. Then identify an owner, preventive step, review point, and response. “Use AI responsibly” is an intention; a named reviewer and a clear escalation rule are operational decisions.

Evaluate vendors against the job to be done

Compare options using the needs of your use case: output quality, ease of use, accessibility, data handling, review controls, support, price structure, and exit options. Do not equate a well-known brand with suitability. For classroom analysis, use supplied fictional vendor profiles or publicly available information. Label unavailable information as unknown, and avoid presenting marketing language as independently established performance.

Keep information use proportionate

For course activities, use fictional or instructor-supplied information and avoid confidential records. In a business proposal, identify what information the service would actually need and what should be excluded. Ask how access, retention, review, and vendor changes would be handled. A business decision-maker should know which questions require institutional, contractual, legal, or specialist review before real adoption.

Worked due-diligence note and risk register

A concise purchasing note might say: “Tool B’s review and export features match our needs, but we have not established retention terms or accuracy for our use case. Test both options with the same fictional fact sheets and request authoritative answers to the missing terms. Retain a manual option until the requirements are met.” Turn that into a small risk register: unsupported product claim → store manager checks each claim against the fact sheet → pause if an unapproved claim reaches the final output; unavailable service → staff use the approved template → record the interruption; inability to retrieve work → confirm export with sample documents before relying on the tool. Each entry names a failure, owner, preventive action, and response. A feature list is not a control unless the business can use it consistently. Price, reputation, and a persuasive demo do not settle suitability.

Apply this lesson across business disciplines
Finance
Compare reporting-tool options using quality, access, retention, export, and complete cost.
Marketing
Evaluate content or research tools against the claims and review needs of the offer.
MBA / Strategy
Choose tools that support the operating model without assuming a famous vendor ensures success.
MIS
Document vendor requirements, ownership, fallback, and exit options in business language.
Operations
Compare service continuity, review burden, and exception handling across options.

Carry this into your decision

Compare like with like. Unknown terms belong on the decision record. Choose a bounded trial, request evidence, or defer; a low advertised price does not settle the purchase.

Fictional business case

Put the ideas to work

Two fictional tools support product-description drafting. Tool A costs less but its supplied profile does not explain retention or export options. Tool B costs more and includes role-based review and document export. Neither profile provides independent evidence of output accuracy. For comparison practice only, assume Tool A costs $18 per month and Tool B $42 per month; these are invented vendor profiles, not actual prices or recommendations. Both can draft a listing from supplied product facts. Tool B’s profile states that work can be exported as a document and a reviewer can approve drafts. Actual quality, the usefulness of these features, and retention terms remain unverified for both. A manual template is the third option.

Discuss the decision

  1. Which unknowns matter most for the proposed business use?
  2. What controls could reduce risk regardless of vendor?
  3. What evidence would justify paying more, or choosing neither option?

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Compare first-review pass rates without treating 12 examples as proof of reliability.
  2. At an assumed 40 listings per month, how much staff time does B release relative to A?
  3. If staff capacity is valued at $24 per hour, does B’s higher price necessarily make it worse?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

First-review pass rates are 75% for A, 91.7% for B, and 100% for the manual template in this fictional exercise. Twelve examples are a small, constructed set; these are not general vendor accuracy claims. B takes two fewer minutes per listing than A, or 80 minutes at 40 listings monthly. That capacity is valued at 80 ÷ 60 × $24 = $32, compared with a $24 subscription premium. The illustrative capacity-value difference is $8 in B’s favor before other costs. It is not cash savings if payroll stays fixed. Quality, retention, access, and exit requirements still matter.

Discussion · make your reasoning visible

What Would You Need to Know Before Buying?

Identify one vendor claim you would investigate before recommending a tool. Explain why it matters, the evidence you would request, and a control the business should retain even if the vendor’s claim is accurate. Write 150–250 words and respond to one classmate in 75–100 words with a specific question or improvement. Distinguish case facts, assumptions, and AI-generated suggestions; disclose any AI assistance. Use fictional or supplied case data. Use the instructor’s calendar for your live offering.

Suggested semester scoring: 5 points, with 3 for the original contribution and 2 for a constructive reply. Follow the arrangements designated for your offering. Independent learners can write a response and compare it with a peer.
