Your capstone

One project. Five snapshots.

Keep the same project record as your evidence and solution improve.

Choose a bounded need with a partner, your organization, or the supplied classroom client. Deliver an actual document kit, decision aid, AI-assisted service, or no-code experience. Someone else must complete its intended task; record any assistance they need.

Use one folder with five sections: need, solution, test, impact and adoption, and handoff. Keep source evidence and versions alongside it. The final synthesis can be a 4–6-page decision brief and 6–8-slide pitch with links to the artifact, calculations, observations, and instructions.

1. Problem and feasible scope

What to produce and how to assess it

Submit or review the first snapshot of the same project record. Show a specific need, the user and responsible decision owner, a traceable evidence log, numerical baseline, three alternatives, a justified AI capability choice, and a bounded charter. Reuse the discovery work rather than write a separate background paper.

A complete classroom case is equivalent to a partner context for practicing these criteria. Label supplied facts, role-play, observations, and assumptions accurately. A compelling technology description without a worthwhile task does not meet the goal.

In an instructor-led offering, obtain scope feedback before substantial building. The rubric weights below divide this milestone; the milestone contributes 15% in the proposed full-course model. Self-paced review does not imply a grade.

2. First usable solution

What to produce and how to assess it

Provide the actual versioned artifact or supervised service kit, its chosen AI capability and operating workflow, three varied bench tests, a brief reviewer access/use check, and a meaningful revision with retest. A document-based service is sufficient when another person can complete the bounded task. A mock-up or demonstration deck alone is not.

Include the source card, input/output examples, review criteria, exception handoff, and manual fallback. Identify the permitted input, AI contribution, checked output, and implementation route. A supplied-output route must label its AI step as simulated; do not claim a live model connection or automation. Record assistance honestly. If another person has not attempted the task, show that requirement as pending rather than substituting an invented result.

3. Pilot and learning

What to produce and how to assess it

Show the predeclared pilot question and measures, actual user-task record, defensible comparison, complete effort and quality, meaningful revision, and retest. Keep supplied benchmark figures separate from what happened with your version.

The result may support continuation, narrowing, or stopping. Grade quality of evaluation and judgment, not a positive AI result. Include the individual explanation near this milestone; together with the later check, it contributes to the individual component recorded under Milestone 5, not a second grade here.

4. Impact and adoption plan

What to produce and how to assess it

Update the same project record with intended impact, complete resource needs, capacity, a base and unfavorable scenario, release readiness, a bounded route to users, and onboarding feedback. The capstone may address an appropriate personal, community, creative, research, or organizational need. Commercialization is optional and is not required for full credit.

Separate observation from forecast, identify omitted effort or costs, and explain a proportionate next decision. Show actual version/access status, intended users and responsible owner, an acceptance task, clear instructions, and support limits. A role-play can demonstrate communication practice; any claimed successful use needs an actual observed task record. Label missing or pending evidence honestly.

5. Demonstration and handoff

What to produce and how to assess it

Demonstrate the actual bounded task and defend the recommendation using the artifact, test/retest evidence, impact/resources, and operating handoff. Provide a concise decision brief and presentation or an accessible equivalent using the offering’s announced arrangements.

This milestone’s internal rubric allocates 60% to the shared demonstration/handoff and 40% to individual mastery. In the full-course model, those contribute 15 and 10 percentage points respectively. The individual evidence comes from the checks near Milestones 3 and 5; do not grade it twice. Every learner explains a calculation, a decision, and a limitation. Self-paced completion does not imply instructor grading or a credential.

Your individual explanation

Teams can share an artifact; every learner must explain a calculation, a design choice, and a limitation. The final milestone’s suggested 25% comprises 15% demonstration/handoff and 10% individual explanation. In a taught offering, two short individual checks around Milestones 3 and 5 provide evidence across the project.

These are proposed weights for a new full offering. Your instructor publishes the actual schedule, feedback windows, and assessment rules. This website does not change an institutional gradebook.

What counts as completion?

  • A specific need supported by traceable evidence.
  • A usable AI-assisted candidate compared with a simpler alternative.
  • An observed user task, a meaningful revision, and a retest.
  • An impact and resource model, usable release package, and supported route to users.
  • A demonstration, limits, operating instructions, and handoff.

Recommend narrowing, revising, or stopping when the evidence supports it. If a non-AI option wins, still evaluate a bounded AI candidate in the classroom; do not impose an inferior option on a partner.
