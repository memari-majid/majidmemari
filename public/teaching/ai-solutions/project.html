Your capstone

One project. Five snapshots.

Keep the same project record as your evidence and solution improve.

Choose a bounded need with a partner, your organization, or the supplied classroom client. Deliver an actual document kit, decision aid, AI-assisted service, or no-code experience. Someone else must complete its intended task; record any assistance they need.

Use one folder with five sections: need, solution, test, impact and adoption, and handoff. Keep source evidence and versions alongside it. The final synthesis can be a 4–6-page decision brief and 6–8-slide pitch with links to the artifact, calculations, observations, and instructions.

1. Problem and feasible scope

What to produce and how to assess it

Submit or review the first snapshot of the same project record. Show a specific need, the user and responsible decision owner, a traceable evidence log, numerical baseline, three alternatives, a justified AI capability choice, and a bounded charter. Reuse the discovery work rather than write a separate background paper.

A complete classroom case is equivalent to a partner context for practicing these criteria. Label supplied facts, role-play, observations, and assumptions accurately. A compelling technology description without a worthwhile task does not meet the goal.

In an instructor-led offering, obtain scope feedback before substantial building. The rubric weights below divide this milestone; the milestone contributes 15% in the proposed full-course model. Self-paced review does not imply a grade.

2. First usable solution

What to produce and how to assess it

Provide the actual versioned artifact or supervised service kit, its chosen AI capability and operating workflow, three varied bench tests, a brief reviewer access/use check, and a meaningful revision with retest. A document-based service is sufficient when another person can complete the bounded task. A mock-up or demonstration deck alone is not.

Include the source card, input/output examples, review criteria, exception handoff, and manual fallback. Identify the permitted input, AI contribution, checked output, and implementation route. A supplied-output route must label its AI step as simulated; do not claim a live model connection or automation. Record assistance honestly. If another person has not attempted the task, show that requirement as pending rather than substituting an invented result.

3. Pilot and learning

What to produce and how to assess it

Show the predeclared pilot question and measures, actual user-task record, defensible comparison, complete effort and quality, meaningful revision, and retest. Keep supplied benchmark figures separate from what happened with your version.

The result may support continuation, narrowing, or stopping. Grade quality of evaluation and judgment, not a positive AI result. The individual explanation near this milestone is ungraded formative preparation. The individual case exam is the sole assessed 40-point individual component within Milestone 5; this earlier explanation contributes no separate score.

4. Impact and adoption plan

What to produce and how to assess it

Update the same project record with intended impact, complete resource needs, capacity, a base and unfavorable scenario, release readiness, a bounded route to users, and onboarding feedback. The capstone may address an appropriate personal, community, creative, research, or organizational need. Commercialization is optional and is not required for full credit.

Separate observation from forecast, identify omitted effort or costs, and explain a proportionate next decision. Show actual version/access status, intended users and responsible owner, an acceptance task, clear instructions, and support limits. A role-play can demonstrate communication practice; any claimed successful use needs an actual observed task record. Label missing or pending evidence honestly.

5. Demonstration and handoff

What to produce and how to assess it

Demonstrate the actual bounded task and defend the recommendation using the artifact, test/retest evidence, impact/resources, and operating handoff. Provide a concise decision brief and presentation or an accessible equivalent using the offering’s announced arrangements.

This milestone’s internal rubric allocates 60% to the shared demonstration/handoff and 40% to the individual case exam. In the full-course model, those contribute 15 and 10 percentage points respectively. The case exam is the sole assessed individual component; earlier explanations are ungraded preparation and are not included again. Every learner explains a calculation, a decision, and a limitation. Self-paced completion does not imply instructor grading or a credential.

Arrange review and revisions in a taught offering

Arrange an actual user attempt early

Use an approved need or the complete fictional Cedar Desk case. Another person can test your usable files or service instructions with safe fictional inputs; no business partner, fee, paid AI account, public launch, or sale is required.

  1. By the end of Unit 4: identify a willing reviewer or request one through Canvas Inbox. The instructor coordinates willing classmates in pairs or triads through existing Canvas communication; this is task testing, not peer grading or another submission. A colleague or friend is also acceptable. Send your version or plan, expected ready date, one safe five-to-ten-minute task, expected behavior and the observation you need. Supply the usable files before the attempt. Use fictional or approved information; do not share private partner or personal data with a classmate.
  2. By the end of Unit 6: confirm who will try the version and when the pilot and retest will happen. Keep the actual attempt, assistance, completion, quality, timing, revision and retest in your existing project log. Pairing alone is not an observation or a guarantee that someone has completed the task.
  3. Facilitated-course backstop: the instructor acknowledges a reviewer request within the two-working-day response target and first arranges a willing classmate or replacement reviewer. For an unresolved access or availability problem, request an instructor appointment. The single-instructor plan for up to 30 learners reserves four appointments of up to 15 minutes per teaching week. Each appointment is one task attempt, including its access check and essential notes; a pilot and its retest require two separately confirmed slots. Actual dates and reviewer coverage must be confirmed before dependent work is due. This is a planning limit, not a promise that the instructor personally tests every project. If all 30 learners needed both attempts from the instructor, 60 slots would take 15 teaching weeks at that rate; the pairing plan must cover the cohort before enrollment.
  4. If a confirmed reviewer is delayed: submit completed parts on time and mark the observation pending. The instructor confirms a replacement or adjusted observation deadline through Canvas, without a penalty caused by reviewer availability. Do not score a delayed observation as missing before its adjusted deadline. While waiting, continue safe bench tests, source checks and classroom-case practice; those do not replace the actual attempt.

Self-review and AI role-play help preparation; they do not establish another person's actual attempt. Leave unobserved events pending rather than inventing them. Independent self-study requires you to arrange a willing reviewer and does not include an instructor review service or a grade.

One optional milestone revision

After feedback, you may submit one optional revised snapshot for each of the five milestones through the same Canvas assignment. The offering's schedule publishes a revision deadline for each milestone. Keep the original version and add a short feedback → change → evidence note. The final reviewed submission counts; this is not another project or a new graded item.

An approved revision of an on-time original receives no additional late penalty. Canvas may show a technical late label on the later timestamp; the approved revision arrangement governs assessment. Any existing penalty on the original submission remains governed by the offering's late-work policy.

If instructor feedback arrives after its published target, the affected milestone revision opportunity extends to at least seven calendar days after the feedback. If that would pass the offering's final grading cutoff, the instructor confirms an individual arrangement through Canvas before closing the assessment. An instructor-arranged reviewer delay is handled through the same private support route, without inventing the missing observation.

The individual case exam uses its separately published optional initial-submission, feedback and final-revision dates. Independent self-study does not include instructor feedback, assessment or promised revision service.

Your individual explanation

Teams can share an artifact; every learner must explain a calculation, a design choice, and a limitation. The final milestone’s suggested 25% comprises 15% demonstration/handoff and 10% individual case exam. Earlier short individual explanations are ungraded preparation; the case exam is the sole assessed 40-point individual component.

These are proposed weights for a new full offering. Your instructor publishes the actual schedule, feedback windows, and assessment rules. This website does not change an institutional gradebook.

What counts as completion?

  • A specific need supported by traceable evidence.
  • A usable AI-assisted candidate compared with a simpler alternative.
  • An observed user task, a meaningful revision, and a retest.
  • An impact and resource model, usable release package, and supported route to users.
  • A demonstration, limits, operating instructions, and handoff.

Recommend narrowing, revising, or stopping when the evidence supports it. If a non-AI option wins, still evaluate a bounded AI candidate in the classroom; do not impose an inferior option on a partner.
