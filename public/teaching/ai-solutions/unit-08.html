Evaluate impact and resources

Does the benefit survive preparation, review, rework, delivery, and support?

Planned guided work including textbook reading: 81–131 minutes, plus project work. This is a design estimate.

Connect a measured task result to a realistic impact estimate

Useful impact can mean more reliable information, access to a service, a shorter queue, better-quality work, reduced effort, or a creative result someone can use. Choose a measure that fits the need and a quality boundary that must be met. Costs and capacity matter in personal and community projects as well as organizations; money alone does not measure every benefit.

The benchmark released one minute per reviewed reply:8 minutes manual versus 7 assisted. Scaling that finding requires assumptions about which replies are eligible, how often the tool is used, whether the result transfers, and what the released time enables. None of those assumptions is established merely by multiplying.

The non-assisted 600 replies remain at 8 minutes each. Complete processing is 600 × 7 +600 × 8 =9,000 minutes, or 150 hours, against 160 hours for 1,200 manual replies. The ten released hours could reduce a backlog or improve service. Those benefits need their own evidence. Do not count capacity value and a hypothetical revenue gain from the same hours twice.

At one minute and $24/hour, each assisted reply releases $0.40 of assigned capacity value. Covering $240 recurring expense requires 600 assisted replies:50% of 1,200. That is a capacity-value break-even threshold, not a cash break-even with unchanged payroll. The costs are planning inputs, not quoted prices for any real product.

Worked explanation and evidence limits

Same monthly service, lower assumed adoption

The fixed challenge keeps 1,200 eligible replies per month, one minute released per assisted reply, $24/hour assigned capacity value, $180 tools, and $60 support. Only planned adoption changes from 50% to 30%. These are hypothetical planning inputs, not observed pilot outcomes or the settings from your separate exploration.

Lower assumed use reduces released time; it does not reduce the fixed recurring expense in this model. The base already has no positive net capacity value. The 30% scenario is below the 50% capacity-value break-even threshold. A bounded test or a revised process may be justified, but neither scenario demonstrates cash savings. Explore another assumption without mixing the comparisons

If extra review reduces the saving to half a minute while adoption remains 50%, 600 assisted replies release five hours: $120 of capacity value against $240 expense, or −$120 net. This is a different sensitivity case from the fixed lower-adoption comparison.

At 75% adoption with the original one-minute gain, 900 replies release 15 hours, valued at $360. Net capacity value is $120/month after the same $240 recurring expense. If a separate one-time setup expense is assumed to be $480, simple value payback is $480 ÷ $120 = 4 months under constant conditions. It is not cash payback when payroll is unchanged and the benefit is only valued time. At the base scenario's zero net value, there is no finite payback under this simple model.

Do not change adoption to 75% merely to make the chart attractive. State what observations would justify it, how support load might change, and whether those extra users resemble the pilot users. Setup costs, unexpected incidents, and a productive destination for the released time also require investigation.

Fictional teaching scenario. A fictional operation handles 1,200 replies per month. At 50% use, the reviewed workflow saves one complete-effort minute per assisted reply. Staff value is $24/hour, recurring tools/support cost $240/month, and payroll is unchanged. Choose the claim.

  1. It saves $240 cash each month
  2. It releases capacity; cash must be calculated separately
  3. All 1,200 replies receive the benefit

Choose one and write why before revealing the evidence.

Reveal evidence and challenge
  • Time: 1,200 × 50% = 600 replies; 600 minutes = 10 hours released.
  • Capacity value: 10 hours × $24 = $240; minus $240 recurring expense = $0 net capacity value.
  • Cash: Unchanged payroll and $240 new recurring expense give −$240 monthly cash change.

Now change use to 30%. Recalculate released hours, net capacity value, and cash change. What decision follows?

Compare with the worked reasoning

At 30%, 360 replies release 360 minutes = 6 hours, valued at $144. Net capacity value is $144 − $240 = −$96/month; recurring cash change stays −$240/month if payroll and other cash flows stay unchanged. The lower-use scenario weakens this value case. A bounded next test should check actual use and complete effort before a broader commitment.
