iExplain

Choose a learning investment before a large commitment

A worthwhile problem does not automatically require AI. Cedar Desk could organize its policy card, provide a checked reply template, or use AI to adapt a draft to a question. Compare these against the same need. A convenient tool with unclear review responsibilities may be a worse first investment than a well-organized source sheet.

First screen for feasibility: permitted information, available tools, a reviewer, and time to test. Then compare value, evidence, effort, and consequences. A score is a recorded judgment, not a measurement of future impact. Explain what evidence would justify changing it. An option that cannot meet a quality boundary does not become acceptable by receiving a high convenience score.

Set one primary task: a coordinator uses ReplyKit to produce a checked reply or an owner-ready exception note. Exclude processing refunds, changing bookings, live customer messages, and owner decisions. A useful scope is small enough to complete and broad enough to include the actual handoff.

David L. Poole and Alan K. Mackworth. Artificial Intelligence: Foundations of Computational Agents. 3rd edition. Cambridge University Press, 2023.

11.3: Conducting a Feasibility Analysis

Michael Laverty and Chris Littel. Entrepreneurship. 1st edition. OpenStax, Rice University, 2020.

Read: Read the opening explanation and Organizational Feasibility Analysis only. Stop at Financial Feasibility Analysis. Do not prepare a business plan or financial statements.

Why this unit: Evaluate whether people, skills and resources can support the proposed scope.

Course connection: A first solution should solve one bounded task well enough for a stated use. Write a minimum acceptable result, not a promise that the AI is always right. Consider whether a simpler checklist, template, search tool, or process change would meet the need without AI.

Course connection: Feasibility is separate from usefulness. A community, personal, research or business solution needs both a worthwhile task and a practicable way to perform it.

Try

?Try / predict

Find the assumption that changes the recommendation

A fictional planning estimate puts manual handling at 8 minutes per reply. An AI draft takes 2 minutes, checking 3, correction 2, and handoff 1. Predict the saving before adding: 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 8. Faster drafting has produced no full-process time gain. If checking falls to 2 minutes without reducing quality, the total becomes 7 and the possible gain is 1 minute. That change needs evidence.

A defensible selection statement

“Compare the organized template and reviewed AI candidate on the same twelve supplied cases. Keep commitments under human control. Select continued use only after checking total effort, material errors, and the coordinator’s ability to use the kit. We have not selected a live system or verified a commercial benefit.”

This statement buys information. It does not hide an unfavorable result. If the simpler option wins, the bounded AI comparison still demonstrates AI use and evidence-based judgment. You may recommend the simpler option instead of deploying the inferior AI candidate externally.

Now consider a tempting additional feature: the tool automatically changes bookings. It expands permissions, consequences, and testing. Ask whether it is necessary for the selected task. Here it is outside scope; a human handoff completes the process without adding that dependency.

A testable scope is a reviewed draft for ordinary questions using a named source version. Exclude exception decisions and sending replies. Compare complete effort with the manual template while requiring supported policy claims. A narrower scope is useful when it preserves a real task and makes evidence inspectable.

How criterion scores are assigned

Each rubric criterion keeps its published maximum points. The instructor judges the evidence against that criterion's Meets the goal and Stronger evidence descriptions and uses these numerical anchors.

Scoring anchors within each criterion's maximum
Evidence levelAnchorWhat the evidence shows
Strong evidence100% of the criterion maximumMeets the goal and demonstrates the criterion's stronger evidence or transfer.
Meets the goal80% of the criterion maximumDemonstrates the stated goal with accurate, usable evidence.
Developing50% of the criterion maximumShows a relevant attempt, but important evidence is incomplete, unreliable, or needs correction.
Not demonstrated0% of the criterion maximumProvides no usable evidence for this criterion.

These are judgment anchors, not automatic all-or-nothing ratings. Intermediate points are allowed within the criterion maximum, with a comment identifying the evidence and remaining gap. For a 25-point criterion the anchors are 25, 20, 12.5 and 0 points. A defensible decision to narrow, revise, or stop can earn strong credit. These anchors do not change criterion maximums, assessment totals, course weights, or previously recorded scores.

A favorable project result is not required; the quality of evidence and judgment matters.
