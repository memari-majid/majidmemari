Choose and scope a solution

Which response fits the need, uncertainty, and resources?

Learn

By the end of this unit

  • Compare current practice, a simpler improvement, and an AI-assisted candidate on the same criteria.
  • Define a bounded task and a decision that evidence can change.

Choose a learning investment before a large commitment

A worthwhile problem does not automatically require AI. Cedar Desk could organize its policy card, provide a checked reply template, or use AI to adapt a draft to a question. Compare these against the same need. A convenient tool with unclear review responsibilities may be a worse first investment than a well-organized source sheet.

First screen for feasibility: permitted information, available tools, a reviewer, and time to test. Then compare value, evidence, effort, and consequences. A score is a recorded judgment, not a measurement of future profit. Explain what evidence would justify changing it. An option that cannot meet a quality boundary does not become acceptable by receiving a high convenience score.

Set one primary task: a coordinator uses ReplyKit to produce a checked reply or an owner-ready exception note. Exclude processing refunds, changing bookings, live customer messages, and owner decisions. A useful scope is small enough to complete and broad enough to include the actual handoff.

Read the experiment-selection principles and the discovery/validation distinction. Name the uncertainty your next test addresses. The article’s suggested experiment cadence is not an added course requirement.

Try

Find the assumption that changes the recommendation

A fictional planning estimate puts manual handling at 8 minutes per reply. An AI draft takes 2 minutes, checking 3, correction 2, and handoff 1. Predict the saving before adding: 2 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 8. Faster drafting has produced no full-process time gain. If checking falls to 2 minutes without reducing quality, the total becomes 7 and the possible gain is 1 minute. That change needs evidence.

A defensible selection statement

“Compare the organized template and reviewed AI candidate on the same twelve supplied cases. Keep commitments under human control. Select continued use only after checking total effort, material errors, and the coordinator’s ability to use the kit. We have not selected a live system or verified a commercial benefit.”

This statement buys information. It does not hide an unfavorable result. If the simpler option wins, the bounded AI comparison still demonstrates AI use and business judgment. You may recommend the simpler option instead of deploying the inferior AI candidate externally.

Now consider a tempting additional feature: the tool automatically changes bookings. It expands permissions, consequences, and testing. Ask whether it is necessary for the selected task. Here it is outside scope; a human handoff completes the business process without adding that dependency.

Apply this step to your chosen problem. Cedar Desk is the supplied classroom case; keep one project throughout the course.

Prepare Milestone 1: problem and feasible scope

Update the same dossier rather than rewrite the discovery material. Add a three-option comparison and a one-page charter: user, buyer/sponsor, need, evidence, current process, task, usable output, permitted inputs, resources, quality boundary, and exclusions.

Write the decision question in a form a small test can inform: “Can a coordinator produce a source-grounded reply or appropriate exception handoff with less complete effort than the current method on the supplied cases?” Record an important uncertainty and how you will investigate it. “Prove that AI is better” presupposes the answer.

For an instructor-led offering, obtain scope feedback before substantial construction; use the published milestone rubric. While feedback is pending, practice with the supplied case. Self-paced learners compare their charter with the worked example and note the limits of self-review. Keep a brief change log explaining one decision that changed after discovery. What will you carry forward?

AI drafting falls from 8 minutes to 2, but checking, correction, and handoff add 6. What business claim is supported?

In your project record, state what you decided, the evidence you used, and one uncertainty to test next. Explain the reasoning in your own words.

Evidence and assessment criteria

Submit or review the first snapshot of the same project record. Show a specific need, the user and buyer/sponsor, a traceable evidence log, numerical baseline, three alternatives, and a bounded charter. Reuse the discovery work rather than write a separate background paper.

A complete classroom case is equivalent to a partner context for practicing these criteria. Label supplied facts, role-play, observations, and assumptions accurately. A compelling technology description without a worthwhile task does not meet the goal.

In an instructor-led offering, obtain scope feedback before substantial building. The rubric weights below divide this milestone; the milestone contributes 15% in the proposed full-course model. Self-paced review does not imply a grade.
