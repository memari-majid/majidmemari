Unit 11 of 12

Make delivery dependable

Who owns quality, exceptions, updates, and continued use?

Learn

By the end of this unit

  • Create operating instructions and a handoff that another person can follow.
  • Respond to changed information or a failure without giving AI unbounded authority.

Planned guided work including textbook reading: 77–127 minutes, plus project work. This is a design estimate.

Responsibility continues after the first useful output

Dependable delivery requires more than a successful demonstration. Sources change, users forget instructions, unusual requests arrive, and a busy reviewer can become the bottleneck. A service needs someone responsible for detecting those conditions and deciding what happens next. A personal tool, community project, creative service, or organizational solution still needs a responsible owner.

Imagine assigning AI three bounded roles: organize permitted facts, prepare a draft, and highlight possible unsupported claims. These roles may help one operator. Asking another AI to check the first does not create independent ground truth; both can miss the same source condition. The responsible person checks consequential claims against the approved evidence and controls release.

Define service measures that inform action: acceptable first-pass outputs, material errors, complete effort, time waiting for review, rework, and continued use. A high output count can hide an expanding queue. A support boundary should state what is included, who handles problems, and when the owner reviews continued use. Avoid unlimited promises that the course project cannot maintain.

A handoff is a transfer of the ability to operate, not just the transfer of a file. The recipient needs the current version, purpose, inputs, source, ordinary procedure, exception rule, access location, limitations, and maintenance contact or role. A classroom version can name a role and explicitly state that no ongoing support is provided.

Delegation — choose the work
Decide whether AI is useful for this task, which step it may assist, and which decisions a person must retain.
Description — make the task clear
Give the permitted inputs, goal, audience, output format, constraints, and a clear stopping rule.
Discernment — inspect the result
Compare the output with sources and success criteria. Check for missing conditions, invented claims, and whether it helps complete the intended task.
Diligence — remain responsible
Own the decision to use or release the result. Protect information, disclose material AI assistance, and arrange human review or escalation where needed.

Try

Handle a source change without guessing

The owner says the cancellation policy has changed but has not supplied approved wording. An old draft is ready to send. Predict the next action. A plausible new policy generated by AI is not an authorized replacement; pause the affected output and obtain the approved version.

Worked recovery sequence

Mark the old output held. Record which source and cases may be affected. Obtain and label the new source, update the response rule, and rerun boundary and exception tests. Ask a reviewer to approve the revised version before use. Preserve a brief change log so users know which version is current.

If an incorrect reply had already been delivered in an actual service, the owner would assess the impact and authorize an appropriate correction. In this classroom scenario, describe that procedure; do not invent a sent correction or customer notification.

Now give your draft instructions to someone unfamiliar with the kit. Ask them to locate the current source, identify who can authorize an exception, and find the fallback. Difficulty finding these items is a handoff defect, even if your demonstration was smooth.

Complete reading route · no AI service needed

Fictional teaching scenario. A separate fictional workflow receives 18 drafts per day, but its owner can review only 12 per day. All consequential replies require review. Choose the operating response.

  1. Auto-send the excess six drafts
  2. Cap or pause intake and route urgent exceptions to an owner
  3. Keep accepting work without a warning

Choose one and write why before revealing the evidence.

Reveal evidence and challenge
  • Queue arithmetic: 18 arrivals − 12 reviews = 6 additional unreviewed drafts per day while those rates persist.
  • Capacity limit: After three such days, 18 additional drafts are waiting, assuming an initially empty queue and no other changes.
  • Source change: The approved source version changes during the backlog; pending drafts need a source check before release.

Write a stop rule and recovery step. Who owns the queue, the source update, and communication about delays?

Compare with the worked reasoning

At unchanged rates the backlog grows by six per day. Cap intake or add approved review capacity; route urgent cases and communicate delays. After a source change, check pending drafts against the new approved version before sending. Name an owner, a queue limit, a fallback, and a restart check. AI drafting speed does not create human review capacity.
