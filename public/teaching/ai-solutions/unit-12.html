iExplain

Make a decision easy to inspect

The final audience needs to understand what the solution does, what you learned, what it costs, and what should happen next. Lead with a proportionate recommendation. Show the task, sources, and review point. Then use the strongest evidence to explain the recommendation rather than narrating every tool you tried.

A concise decision brief can use six sections: need and alternatives; implemented solution; actual test and revision; impact, resources, and capacity; route to users and operating plan; recommendation and handoff. Keep the artifact, detailed records, and calculations in supporting files. Reuse corrected earlier work and remove claims that later evidence contradicted.

Model opening: evidence-supported caution

“I recommend a bounded next test of the reviewed ReplyKit, not a broad rollout yet. The supplied benchmark shows twelve acceptable replies with less complete effort than the manual comparison. That is classroom case evidence, not our own deployment or evidence of wider use. The monthly base scenario has zero net capacity value and negative cash change. Our next decision depends on observed use, review effort, and quality.”

Adapt the structure to your actual project. Replace supplied evidence with your observed record where available, keep the distinction visible, and do not copy an event you did not perform.

?Try / predict

Rehearse the decision, then change one assumption

Give a short demonstration: locate the source, enter an ordinary or exception question, produce the useful output, show the human check, and locate the record. A video or screenshots can support access needs, but the usable artifact and actual task evidence must still be available.

Ask a reviewer to challenge one assumption: “What if adoption is 30%?”, “What if review adds six minutes?”, or “What if the user prefers the existing template?” Answer with the relevant calculation or evidence limit. A clear “we have not established that” is more defensible than an invented answer.

Worked changed-assumption answer

For the supplied monthly scenario,30% of 1,200 is 360 assisted replies. One minute each releases six hours, valued at $144. After $240 recurring expense, net capacity value is − $96. With unchanged payroll, recurring cash change remains − $240. This weakens the value case; it does not prove that every possible use is worthless.

=Worked reasoning

A defensible recommendation is a bounded next test of a reviewed workflow. The supplied comparison shows 12.5% lower complete effort on these twelve cases. Proceeding depends on confirming that both methods met the same quality threshold: acceptable-output counts are not provided here. The changed-use scenario weakens the value case. These are fictional course data, not the team’s observed deployment. Evidence and assessment criteria

Demonstrate the actual bounded task and defend the recommendation using the artifact, test/retest evidence, impact/resources, and operating handoff. Provide a concise decision brief and presentation or an accessible equivalent using the offering’s announced arrangements.

This milestone’s internal rubric allocates 60% to the shared demonstration/handoff and 40% to the individual case exam. In the full-course model, those contribute 15 and 10 percentage points respectively. The case exam is the sole assessed individual component; earlier explanations are ungraded preparation and are not included again. Every learner explains a calculation, a decision, and a limitation. Self-paced completion does not imply instructor grading or a credential.

How criterion scores are assigned

Each rubric criterion keeps its published maximum points. The instructor judges the evidence against that criterion's Meets the goal and Stronger evidence descriptions and uses these numerical anchors.

Scoring anchors within each criterion's maximum
Evidence levelAnchorWhat the evidence shows
Strong evidence100% of the criterion maximumMeets the goal and demonstrates the criterion's stronger evidence or transfer.
Meets the goal80% of the criterion maximumDemonstrates the stated goal with accurate, usable evidence.
Developing50% of the criterion maximumShows a relevant attempt, but important evidence is incomplete, unreliable, or needs correction.
Not demonstrated0% of the criterion maximumProvides no usable evidence for this criterion.

These are judgment anchors, not automatic all-or-nothing ratings. Intermediate points are allowed within the criterion maximum, with a comment identifying the evidence and remaining gap. For a 25-point criterion the anchors are 25, 20, 12.5 and 0 points. A defensible decision to narrow, revise, or stop can earn strong credit. These anchors do not change criterion maximums, assessment totals, course weights, or previously recorded scores.

A favorable project result is not required; the quality of evidence and judgment matters.
