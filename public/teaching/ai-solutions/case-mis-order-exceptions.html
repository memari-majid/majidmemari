MIS · application case

Build an Order-Exception Service Kit

Inconsistent triage and incomplete handoffs cause rework and inaccurate customer promises. A high overall routing score can hide costly missed exceptions.

Fictional supplied case · optional application

Understand the problem

Northline Supply is a fictional wholesaler whose staff must distinguish routine order-status questions from exceptions needing review. The proposed MIS solution is a usable information and service kit that can be run manually. Operations students can use the same case to study service quality, workload, and handoffs. This illustrative alternative uses the same milestones as the semester capstone. The confirmed brief and available resources determine the final scope. Complete a controlled partner or classroom deployment of the usable kit and record an actual user task.

Inconsistent triage and incomplete handoffs cause rework and inaccurate customer promises. A high overall routing score can hide costly missed exceptions.

People involved

  • User: order-support employee handling status questions.
  • Affected person: customer waiting for an accurate update.
  • Reviewer: operations lead authorized to handle exceptions.
  • Sponsor: manager responsible for service quality and staff capacity.

Discover the need

Supplied evidence

  • Supplied fictional employee response: “I need to know when a question is routine and what information to send to the supervisor.”
  • Supplied fictional supervisor response: “Extra reviews take time, but a missed exception can create a false promise.”
  • Supplied fictional customer response: “Tell me what is known and what still needs confirmation.”
  • The supplied policy allows staff to repeat a confirmed order status. A promised delivery date, refund, or substitution requires an explicit approved source or supervisor decision.

Questions to investigate

  • Which cases require human review and what information must accompany them?
  • What error consequences differ between unnecessary escalation and a missed exception?
  • What would make the kit more useful than a static policy document?

Design the AI-assisted solution

  1. Use a fictional request and its approved order-status facts as input.
  2. Apply a plain-language triage checklist and record unknown information.
  3. Draft a source-based response or a complete handoff note for the supervisor.
  4. Approve the final response and log whether the case was routine, escalated, corrected, or unresolved.

The role of AI

Help organize the request, suggest a draft, and check for missing information. It does not decide customer entitlements or make unapproved promises.

A reasonable non-AI alternative

A manual triage checklist and response-template library.

Human review

A person checks status facts and escalation, authorizes exceptions, and approves customer-facing commitments. No software integration, API, or live order data is needed.

Build and deliver an artifact

Order-Exception Service Kit

A triage decision table, three reviewed response/handoff examples, a small case log, and an operating checklist.

What the usable package contains

  • A plain-language rule table distinguishing confirmed routine information from exceptions.
  • A routine status response, a missing-date handoff, and an unapproved-refund request response.
  • A log with request type, evidence source, decision, reviewer, and outcome.
  • A short adoption guide showing how staff use the kit and report confusing cases.

Acceptance checks

  • Routine answers contain only approved status facts.
  • Unknown delivery dates and unauthorized refund/substitution requests are escalated.
  • The handoff names the question, known facts, missing information, and responsible reviewer.
  • Error counts use the correct denominators and the supplied fictional comparison is not presented as observed deployment performance.

Business numbers · interpret the evidence

Higher overall accuracy is not always the lower-cost policy

Fictional teaching data; not real customers, forecasts, transactions, or pilot observations. A constructed set of 100 requests contains 40 true exceptions and 60 routine cases. Each flagged request uses $3 of staff review; a missed exception has an assumed $25 consequence; an unnecessary escalation adds $2 of friction beyond staff time.

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Calculate overall correct-routing rates and missed-exception rates.
  2. Calculate modeled staff, missed-issue, and friction costs for each rule.
  3. Which rule is preferable under these assumptions, and what still needs investigation?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

Starting correct routing is (30+50)/100=80%; revised is (36+42)/100=78%. Missed-exception rates are 10/40=25% and 4/40=10%. Starting modeled cost is 40×$3 + 10×$25 + 10×$2 = $390. Revised cost is 54×$3 + 4×$25 + 18×$2 = $298, or $92 lower despite lower overall accuracy. The preference depends on the assumed error costs, supplied labels, review capacity, and actual service effects. These are fictional comparisons, not measured operational savings.

Run a bounded pilot

Can staff use the kit to handle routine information and escalate consequential exceptions with a manageable review burden?

Plan

  • Construct the rule table and three reviewed examples using the supplied policy.
  • Test a routine confirmed-status request, a request for an unknown delivery date, and a refund request with no authorization.
  • Use the supplied count table to compare policies, then vary one error-cost assumption to examine the decision.
  • Record a kit revision and a short adoption/support plan; external deployment is optional, but a controlled classroom delivery and user task are required.
  • Make the versioned kit available to an approved partner or classroom user, verify access, record a defined task and actual feedback, revise, and provide a short handoff. This is the required controlled deployment; no public release is necessary.

Success measures

  • All three artifact tests preserve source facts and route exceptions appropriately.
  • The handoff contains enough information for a supervisor to act.
  • The proposed review burden and error consequences are explicit and fit the service promise.
  • A partner or classroom recipient can access the artifact and complete the agreed task; the record accurately describes the outcome, including failures.

When to pause or stop

  • A final response invents an approved date, refund, or substitution.
  • A policy reduces a headline metric while making a consequential error or staff workload unacceptable.

Make the adoption or business decision

Value inside an organization

A more consistent service process, clearer information handoffs, and a testable quality/workload tradeoff.

A possible venture

An optional training and service-design kit for small operations teams, with a hypothetical offer, acquisition route, and support boundary. It need not become a software company or real paid service.

Assumptions to test

  • Whether the supplied categories reflect the organization’s actual exceptions.
  • Costs and consequences of errors and the available review capacity.
  • Training, ongoing maintenance, and staff use of the kit.
  • Whether a simpler checklist achieves the desired result.
