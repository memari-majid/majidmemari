What you will be able to do

  • Demonstrate a usable AI-assisted business solution and its controlled deployment/user-test evidence.
  • Explain the need, customer or sponsor, acquisition/adoption, selling/approval, delivery, economics, and limits.
  • Defend a proportionate next decision and hand off the artifact with clear ownership and support boundaries.

Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

Present a defensible recommendation

The final capstone connects a business need to an implemented solution, controlled deployment, user-test evidence, numerical analysis, operating plan, and sales or adoption recommendation. Demonstrate what the user can actually do and explain the role of AI assistance and human review. Your conclusion may be to continue, revise, or stop expansion; the grade depends on the quality of implementation, evidence, reasoning, and communication, not sales revenue or enthusiasm for AI. Internal finance, marketing, MIS, and operations solutions are as valid as a venture. No actual public launch, commercial transaction, or external partner is required.

Use the artifact to show the experience

Show the final usable document product, reporting kit, form/template process, customer-service pack, or manually operated AI-assisted service. Explain where the controlled version was delivered or shared and show the user-test record. A screenshot or storyboard can help explain the experience, but a mockup or pitch deck alone is insufficient. Custom code, a public application, and a live technical demonstration are unnecessary; the agreed no-code business task must have been implemented and tried by a partner or classroom user.

Answer questions and close the loop

During Q&A, connect answers to the implemented artifact, actual pilot observations, supplied scenario analysis, and remaining assumptions. Submit a consistent report, deck, artifact, deployment/test record, and concise handoff through the existing assignments. State which outcomes were observed and which remain hypotheses. Explain who owns future use, corrections, maintenance, and access, or why the classroom pilot ends here. A clear stop recommendation is legitimate after honest implementation and testing.

Worked executive decision record

A final decision record can state: “We implemented a reviewed listing kit and shared version 2 with a classroom test user. The user completed the agreed listing task using supplied fictional facts; the record identifies the actual corrections and revision. We recommend a limited next step because demand and the economic assumptions remain unverified.” Replace those example details with what actually occurred. Name the decision, evidence, uncertainty, owner, and stop condition. Do not claim a deployed application, real customer sale, or realized savings unless those events actually happened and are appropriate to disclose. A controlled classroom deployment proves only what its task and evidence support.

Fictional business case

Put the ideas to work

A fictional decision-maker has reviewed your proposal and asks: “What exactly are you asking us to approve, what would we learn, and what would make you stop?” The decision-maker is willing to fund a small learning exercise but does not want unsupported promises.

Discuss the decision

  1. What is the smallest useful decision request?
  2. Which evidence best supports it?
  3. What uncertainty and stop condition should remain visible?

Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

Neither the time rule nor the quality rule is met, and the eight invented practice rows are not a completed 20-case pilot. A defensible classroom recommendation is to revise the review process and test the consequential assumption before considering expansion. State the owner, the limited next comparison, the measures, and the conditions that would change the decision. Faster performance on some rows is useful to inspect but does not override a required quality boundary. The numbers support reasoning about a hypothetical decision, not a claim that an actual business should launch.

Workshop · create an artifact

Deliver the final recommendation

  1. 1Finalize the 4–6-page business report, excluding a short appendix.
  2. 2Submit the six- to eight-slide deck, implemented artifact, deployment/user-test record, and concise handoff to the existing presentation assignment; keep the report within 4–6 pages.
  3. 3Deliver a seven-minute presentation followed by approximately three minutes of instructor/class Q&A, according to class arrangements.
  4. 4Demonstrate the usable solution, summarize the actual controlled test, and state the sales/adoption recommendation, key uncertainty, and condition that would change it.
  5. 5Complete the final discussion by reflecting on a decision that changed during the course.
  6. 6Use this module’s data table to calculate and interpret the requested quantities. Keep counts, denominators, units, and uncertainty visible; incorporate the relevant table or small figure into the existing artifact rather than creating another submission.

Discussion · make your reasoning visible

Course Takeaways and Business Judgment

Explain one way your thinking about AI and business changed. Use a concrete decision from your project, identify the evidence that mattered, and describe how you would apply that lesson to another business problem. Write 150–250 words and respond to one classmate in 75–100 words with a specific question or improvement. Distinguish case facts, assumptions, and AI-generated suggestions; disclose any AI assistance. Use fictional or supplied case data. Use the instructor’s calendar for your live offering.

Suggested semester scoring: 5 points, with 3 for the original contribution and 2 for a constructive reply. Follow the arrangements designated for your offering. Independent learners can write a response and compare it with a peer.
