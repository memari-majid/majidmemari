What you will be able to do

  • Ask neutral discovery questions and interpret feedback within its evidence limits.
  • Calculate simple response rates and explain small-sample sensitivity and sampling bias.
  • Revise a tangible business concept without treating simulated interest as validated demand.

Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

Investigate the problem before selling the idea

Questions such as “Would you love an AI assistant?” invite agreeable but weak evidence. Ask how the person handles the task now, what is difficult, what consequences follow, and what alternatives they have tried. Avoid leading language and promises. In this course, classmate role-play or instructor-supplied case responses are sufficient; real customer outreach is not mandatory. After discovery, practice a short sales or internal-approval conversation: confirm the problem, ask about fit and constraints, show the artifact, explain scope and price or effort, respond to one objection, and agree on a next step. Do not force a close when the fit is weak. Role-play with the supplied responses is enough.

Interpret feedback within its limits

A role-play response, a fictional case, and a real interview are different evidence sources. Label each accurately. Record both favorable and unfavorable feedback and separate what the participant said from your interpretation. Three positive comments do not establish a market. Useful evidence may show that the problem is smaller than expected, the proposed experience is confusing, or a simpler solution is preferable. Always show a response count and denominator beside a percentage. One response moves a five-person group by 20 percentage points; a larger group is less sensitive to one response but can still be biased by recruitment or leading wording. Interest, an intention to buy, and an actual purchase are different outcomes. No formal statistical test is required to recognize these limits.

Revise a concrete concept

Use a storyboard, mockup, sample output, or manual service walkthrough to give feedback something specific to address. Ask what the reviewer thinks the product does, what is useful, what is unclear, and what would prevent adoption. Document one meaningful change and why it follows from the evidence. Use this feedback to strengthen the proposal and improve the usable business artifact; a custom application is unnecessary. Keep discovery and selling distinct: questions should first help you understand the problem; a later proposal presents a specific offer. A positive role-play reaction is not a commitment. Record what objection changed the offer and what evidence would justify a real launch or purchase. Refine the usable version and the planned deployment from this feedback. Discovery evidence can remain simulated, but the final user test must truthfully record someone using the implemented artifact in the approved partner or classroom setting.

Worked evidence synthesis: revise without claiming validation

An evidence table can use four columns: response, challenged assumption, implication, next question. For supplied Retailer A, “checking takes longer than drafting” challenges drafting as the main bottleneck; the next question is what makes checking difficult. B’s existing templates challenge differentiation; ask what those templates fail to provide. C’s seasonal interest suggests a narrower use occasion, while the concern about claims reinforces the need for review; ask what an acceptable review would look like. A justified revision is a reviewed seasonal listing kit with a visible claim-check record, compared against the retailer’s existing template. The conclusion is “the concept is more specific and its assumptions are clearer,” not “three customers validated demand.” You may use this complete supplied-case route. For a different business, label any adapted responses as fictional scenario material and explain which assumptions they help you examine.

Apply this lesson across business disciplines
Finance
Use neutral questions to discover what makes a financial briefing useful or difficult to trust.
Marketing
Separate awareness, qualified interest, stated intent, and purchase evidence.
MBA / Strategy
Rehearse a sales conversation, respond to an objection, and seek a proportionate next step.
MIS
Test whether staff understand the proposed workflow and where approval belongs.
Operations
Ask users about actual exceptions and compare the proposed service with current workarounds.

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Calculate each rate and state exactly what it measures.
  2. How much does the interest rate change if one positive response becomes negative in the first two rows?
  3. Why can none of these fictional rows establish actual customer demand?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

The rates are 80% expressed interest, 70% expressed interest, and 15% purchase choices. Changing one positive response changes the first rate by 1 ÷ 5 = 20 percentage points, but the second by 1 ÷ 40 = 2.5 points. The larger denominator is less sensitive to one response; it does not automatically make sampling representative or remove leading-question bias. Interest and a purchase choice measure different behavior. All rows are fictional, so they are practice in statistical interpretation, not evidence about a real market. For real data, record recruitment, question wording, context, and limitations before generalizing.

Workshop · create an artifact

Prepare the Business Proposal

  1. 1Write five neutral discovery questions tied to the assumptions in your business model.
  2. 2Use three clearly labeled role-play responses or the three supplied ListingLift responses. For another business, you may adapt them as explicitly fictional scenario material; real outreach is optional.
  3. 3Summarize each response, the assumption it challenges, the implication, and the next question in a short evidence table. Preserve contradictions and evidence limits.
  4. 4Revise the artifact and business model after one material finding. Rehearse a short offer conversation addressing one objection and a proportionate next step; label it as role-play.
  5. 5Combine the problem, evidence, concept, business model, and next validation step into a concise proposal.
  6. 6Use this module’s data table to calculate and interpret the requested quantities. Keep counts, denominators, units, and uncertainty visible; incorporate the relevant table or small figure into the existing artifact rather than creating another submission.

Discussion · make your reasoning visible

What Did the Evidence Change?

Describe one assumption challenged by the discovery exercise and the change you made to your concept. Label the source of the feedback and explain what remains unvalidated. Write 150–250 words and respond to one classmate in 75–100 words with a specific question or improvement. Distinguish case facts, assumptions, and AI-generated suggestions; disclose any AI assistance. Use fictional or supplied case data. Use the instructor’s calendar for your live offering.

Suggested semester scoring: 5 points, with 3 for the original contribution and 2 for a constructive reply. Follow the arrangements designated for your offering. Independent learners can write a response and compare it with a peer.
