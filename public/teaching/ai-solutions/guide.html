What this course is about

This course is about building AI-assisted solutions to real-world problems, including useful products, services, and the foundations of a new business. It welcomes students across finance, accounting, marketing, MBA and management, management information systems, and operations. Evaluate opportunities across financial planning, marketing, customer experience, operations, and decision support. Work with ready-made AI tools, verify their outputs, create usable sample products, and use statistics, tables, figures, and business numbers to test assumptions and present a clear recommendation.

No programming or advanced mathematics is required. Use ordinary arithmetic and a calculator; the emphasis is interpretation and business judgment. Your artifacts may include business briefs, sample AI-assisted outputs, service storyboards, slide mock-ups, simple financial estimates, and presentations. A software application is optional; a usable deliverable and a realistic user test make the solution concrete.

From a problem to a useful solution

  1. Discover: understand who has the problem and what evidence shows it matters.
  2. Design: choose a useful role for AI and define what a person reviews.
  3. Build: create a usable output, product kit, repeatable service, or no-code experience.
  4. Test: use feedback, tables, figures, and simple numbers to improve it.
  5. Deliver: explain how the solution creates value, operates responsibly, and could become an internal service or a viable business.

A repeatable weekly rhythm

  1. Prepare: read one focused resource and identify a question.
  2. Discuss: connect the seminar concepts with a fictional business case.
  3. Make: create a small business artifact in the guided workshop.
  4. Evaluate: check the evidence, assumptions, risks, and business value.
  5. Reflect: contribute to the discussion and improve the cumulative project.

The course contains 16 core modules and an optional final reflection. Your instructor provides the schedule, meeting arrangements, submission process, and any offering-specific policies. This portable site does not impose calendar dates.

Learning outcomes

  • Frame a specific business problem and compare AI and non-AI alternatives.
  • Use or critically evaluate AI-assisted business work with clear instructions and checks.
  • Build a usable AI-assisted product or service without programming, deliver it in a controlled pilot, and evaluate its use.
  • Gather or interpret evidence and distinguish assumptions from validated findings.
  • Evaluate risks, vendor claims, human responsibilities, and adoption readiness.
  • Interpret business tables, charts, counts, rates, averages, and uncertainty; distinguish a numerical difference from a causal claim.
  • Estimate business value using explicit benefits, costs, and sensitivity analysis.
  • Present an evidence-based sales or adoption pitch, with economics, limitations, and an operating handoff.

Use a modern browser and ordinary document or presentation tools. A calculator or simple spreadsheet can support financial estimates and comparisons. Evidence, integrity, and responsible AI

  • Use fictional, supplied, or public non-sensitive information. Do not upload confidential employer, customer, or personal records.
  • Name the AI tool, explain its purpose, include a representative prompt, and state how you checked and changed the output.
  • You are responsible for claims, calculations, citations, and recommendations. Verify important claims against original sources.
  • Label assumptions, simulated interviews, role-play, and AI-generated personas accurately. They do not establish real customer demand.
  • A polished answer is not enough. Assessment rewards reasoning, evidence, revision, and business judgment.

Common questions

What counts as a digital product?

A usable content kit, decision aid, repeatable service, or no-code experience. Storyboards help develop the concept; the final project includes a delivered artifact or service, a controlled user test, and an operating handoff.

Do I have to recruit real customers?

No. Use supplied case evidence or clearly labeled classroom concept tests. Voluntary real feedback can be useful when appropriate; never describe simulated feedback as a real interview.

Can I study independently?

Yes. Read every lesson, use the simulations, answer the knowledge checks, and export your notes. For graded credit, follow the enrollment and submission arrangements provided by your institution and instructor.

Where do I submit work?

This website does not collect assignments or discussion posts. Your instructor provides the submission destination and deadlines for the live offering.

Can I print the lessons?

Use the print button on any module or assessment, or open the complete printable course. Your browser can print on paper or save a PDF.

What if an AI tool or reading is unavailable?

Use the supplied case and manual-analysis route, then contact the instructor if a required reading is inaccessible. No task requires a paid tool. Sources are linked directly so their latest access terms are visible.
