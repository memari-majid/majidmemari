Build the first usable version

Can another person use what we made?

Learn

By the end of this unit

  • Construct a usable no-code route from permitted input through an explicit AI step and human check to an output or handoff.
  • Test varied inputs, record defects, and make a traceable revision.

Planned guided work including textbook reading: 77–127 minutes, plus project work. This is a design estimate.

Construct ReplyKit, one usable part at a time

A product can be a set of dependable working documents. It becomes usable when another person knows what to enter, what to do, what result to expect, and when to ask for help. For this first version, a document editor and calculator are enough. A shared folder is convenient, but you can hand over a complete file without a new account.

  1. Create a Start page. Name ReplyKit version 1, its intended coordinator, the task, and its limits. Tell the user to read the source card before drafting.
  2. Copy the approved source card. Preserve S1–S6 and the version. Keep it beside the response, not hidden behind the author’s explanation.
  3. Make an intake table. Include case reference, customer question, relevant supplied facts, missing facts, and proposed policy source. Use fictional information.
  4. Make a draft-and-evidence table. Use columns for the proposed reply, material claim, source, and check result. Paste the bounded instruction from Unit 1 into the working file.
  5. Add the review checklist. Check source consistency, boundary conditions, unsupported promises, useful next step, and correct handoff. Provide approve/revise/hold choices.
  6. Add a delivery record. Record version, permitted recipient, actual delivery status, reviewer, and unresolved issue. Leave completion fields blank until an action occurs.

You have now built the service’s inputs, working process, quality control, and output. Test the complete route rather than spending all your time formatting its front page. A form, spreadsheet, or visual no-code tool can replace a document later if it makes the task easier; it does not change the evidence standard.

Worked ordinary input and completed output

Input: “I want to cancel. My workshop starts in 48 hours.” Source: S2. A supplied flawed draft promises a refund. The reviewer replaces it with: “Your request is within the 24-to-under 72-hour window, so the policy provides credit valid for 90 days. A coordinator must confirm the booking details and next step; this draft does not mean a credit has already been issued.”

Review result: Correct window; no unsupported refund; credit duration retained; no false completed action. Record: Approved classroom draft; no external delivery. If the time until the session were unknown, ask for it before selecting the rule.

Your continuing case: Cedar Desk

Fictional teaching case. Cedar Desk is a booking and customer-support service for small workshop businesses. Its coordinators answer questions about bookings, cancellations, rescheduling, and special requests. The operations owner wants dependable replies without unnecessary checking or repeated clarification. Your project is ReplyKit: a small source-grounded reply service that turns a permitted question into a checked response or a useful human handoff. It can run with documents, a calculator, and an available AI assistant or the supplied draft. No application or live customer account is needed.

The user is a booking coordinator. The responsible decision owner is the operations owner. A customer receives the final approved reply. Those are three different roles. The owner has agreed only to a classroom exploration in this fictional case, not to a purchase, a live integration, or a commercial launch. A complete usable example

Evidence and assessment criteria

Provide the actual versioned artifact or supervised service kit, its chosen AI capability and operating workflow, three varied bench tests, a brief reviewer access/use check, and a meaningful revision with retest. A document-based service is sufficient when another person can complete the bounded task. A mock-up or demonstration deck alone is not.

Include the source card, input/output examples, review criteria, exception handoff, and manual fallback. Identify the permitted input, AI contribution, checked output, and implementation route. A supplied-output route must label its AI step as simulated; do not claim a live model connection or automation. Record assistance honestly. If another person has not attempted the task, show that requirement as pending rather than substituting an invented result.

How criterion scores are assigned

Each rubric criterion keeps its published maximum points. The instructor judges the evidence against that criterion's Meets the goal and Stronger evidence descriptions and uses these numerical anchors.

Scoring anchors within each criterion's maximum
Evidence levelAnchorWhat the evidence shows
Strong evidence100% of the criterion maximumMeets the goal and demonstrates the criterion's stronger evidence or transfer.
Meets the goal80% of the criterion maximumDemonstrates the stated goal with accurate, usable evidence.
Developing50% of the criterion maximumShows a relevant attempt, but important evidence is incomplete, unreliable, or needs correction.
Not demonstrated0% of the criterion maximumProvides no usable evidence for this criterion.

These are judgment anchors, not automatic all-or-nothing ratings. Intermediate points are allowed within the criterion maximum, with a comment identifying the evidence and remaining gap. For a 25-point criterion the anchors are 25, 20, 12.5 and 0 points. A defensible decision to narrow, revise, or stop can earn strong credit. These anchors do not change criterion maximums, assessment totals, course weights, or previously recorded scores.

A favorable project result is not required; the quality of evidence and judgment matters.
