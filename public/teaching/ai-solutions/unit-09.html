iExplain

Turn a working prototype into a usable release

Your first version worked under test. Now prepare a limited release that another person can locate, understand, and use. A release can be a clearly organized file, an approved shared folder, a visual no-code form, or a supervised service. Public hosting and an autonomous agent are not required. Select the simplest format that fits the task and permitted information.

ReplyKit's release package includes the current source card, intake/review templates, twelve worked cases, a 30-minute handoff, and one revision. The coordinator keeps control of commitments. For another project, the package might be a research extraction sheet with source checks, a creative draft kit with usage instructions, or a community resource helper with an exception route. These are transfer examples; continue your existing capstone.

Prepare within the available resources

Suppose a pilot has a $180 resource-value allowance: a planning boundary for combining assigned effort value and stated cash expense, not a sale price or an all-cash budget. Preparation takes 3 hours, review 1 hour, and handoff 0.5 hour: 4.5 hours. At an assigned $24/hour, that is $108 of valued time. Add $12 incremental tool expense: $120 of stated resource value used. The arithmetic is $180 − $108 − $12 = $60 unallocated resource value.

That $60 is not cash remaining, profit, or evidence that a person will pay. The $108 is valued time and may not be a cash payment. The model has not included every possible setup, accessibility, support, or maintenance requirement. Record those omissions before calling the allowance sufficient. A budget is one constraint; usefulness, quality, access, and consequences still matter.

If the same person has 15 hours available this month to prepare, review, and hand off complete packages, 4.5 hours per package permits three whole packages: 13.5 hours, leaving 1.5 hours. A fourth needs 18 hours and exceeds the limit. A large list of interested users does not create reviewer capacity.

=Worked reasoning

The additional hour consumes $24 of valued time, reducing the unallocated resource-value allowance from $60 to $36. It also reduces capacity from 3 to 2 complete supported releases. The same 15 hours hold three 4.5-hour releases with 1.5 hours unused, or two 5.5-hour releases with 4 hours unused; a third would need 16.5 hours and exceed the limit by 1.5 hours. Neither remainder is cash remaining or profit. This narrow scenario excludes setup and recurring costs, which still need separate estimates. Capacity does not establish that anyone can successfully use the solution.

Use it in your project: Name one extra request outside your release plan and show how it changes effort, resources and the support commitment.

14.1: Types of Resources

Michael Laverty and Chris Littel. Entrepreneurship. 1st edition. OpenStax, Rice University, 2020.

Read: Read the opening explanation and the first paragraph under Tangible Resources. Then read the opening paragraph under Intangible Resources. Skip equipment shopping lists, funding, patent/legal advice and linked activities.

Why this unit: Identify different resource categories before promising that a solution can be delivered.

Course connection: A release needs clear instructions, required inputs, a bounded purpose, a checkable output, and a route for help or stopping. An AI-generated explanation is another output to verify; it is not independent evidence that a recommendation is correct.

Course connection: A file or AI subscription alone does not supply the time, knowledge, permissions and support needed for use. The course resource allowance is an original planning example, not a textbook accounting measure.

Correct an unsupported promise and a difficult start

The proposed Start page says: “Save 75% of labor costs with automatic service.” It hides the source card and does not explain where to enter a question. Identify one evidence problem and one usability problem before opening the worked revision.

A release statement and acceptance check that fit the evidence

“ReplyKit prepares a reviewed draft or exception note for one approved workshop policy. Begin with the source card, then enter a fictional question in the intake table. A person checks the draft before use. The package includes the working files, a handoff, and one revision; it does not change bookings or guarantee savings.”

The unchecked benchmark uses 75% less supplied processing time but contains three material errors. The reviewed comparison shows 12.5% less complete effort in the same set; neither measures a guaranteed reduction in labor expense. Put the source beside the working table, label the input, and ask a reviewer to complete an ordinary task and find the fallback without coaching. Record the assistance actually needed.

Check the planned release against the $180 allowance and 15-hour monthly boundary. Keep the $60 unallocated resource value distinct from the 1.5 hours remaining after three deliveries. They measure different things. If additional support is needed, update the resource estimate rather than silently assuming it is free.

Reveal evidence and challenge
≡Supplied evidence
  • Resources: Tools: $120 cash. Staff time: 5 × $24 = $120 in valued effort.
  • Allowance: The $300 is a planning limit in resource value, not a sales receipt or cash account.
  • Capacity challenge: A separate workload check finds only 3 staff hours available before the proposed release date.

The resource arithmetic fits the allowance, but capacity does not fit the date. What would you narrow, delay, or reassign?

Compare with the worked reasoning
=Worked reasoning

Total resource value is $240, leaving $60 unallocated. Actual new tool spending is $120; staff time valuation is not automatically another cash payment. The 5-hour need exceeds 3 available hours by 2 hours. Narrow scope, delay, or obtain approved capacity, then recheck quality and ownership. Budget fit alone does not establish release readiness.
