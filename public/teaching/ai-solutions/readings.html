Your free textbook reading plan

Short textbook readings support the same twelve-unit path. No book purchase, access code, programming background, or full-book sequence is required. Read the named scope of each listed source, then choose one idea or prompt for the project update you already submit.

Artificial Intelligence: Foundations of Computational Agents ↗
David L. Poole and Alan K. Mackworth. 3rd edition, Cambridge University Press, 2023. Primary academic AI foundation; only the listed conceptual passages are assigned. Free official author-hosted HTML, available with the publisher's permission.
Entrepreneurship ↗
Michael Laverty and Chris Littel. 1st edition, OpenStax, Rice University, 2020. Business companion for selected principles of needs, feasibility, resources, and adoption. Free official online textbook; no paid access code is required.
Introductory Statistics 2e ↗
Barbara Illowsky and Susan Dean. 2nd edition, OpenStax, Rice University, 2023. Quantitative companion for the specific statistics used in the course. Free official online textbook; no paid access code is required.

The AI text is an academic foundation; the course supplies its own current-tool guidance, no-code implementation activities, fictional cases, simulations, and assessment.

Unit 1, 4, 11

The AI Fluency Framework — short guide

Open the short guide here. Identify one decision about delegation, one instruction, one output check, and one responsibility in your task. No account or external PDF reader is needed for this course explanation.

Read the short guide here

Four habits for working with AI. This original course explanation applies the AI Fluency Framework to your project; it is not a reproduction of the publisher's one-page guide.

Delegation — choose the work
Decide whether AI is useful for this task, which step it may assist, and which decisions a person must retain.
Description — make the task clear
Give the permitted inputs, goal, audience, output format, constraints, and a clear stopping rule.
Discernment — inspect the result
Compare the output with sources and success criteria. Check for missing conditions, invented claims, and whether it helps complete the intended task.
Diligence — remain responsible
Own the decision to use or release the result. Protect information, disclose material AI assistance, and arrange human review or escalation where needed.

Apply to your task: Write one AI-assisted step, one instruction, one output check, and one responsibility you keep. Use your existing project record; this is optional practice, not another submission.

Framework attribution: Rick Dakan, Joseph Feller, and Anthropic, The AI Fluency Framework (2025). The definitions above are course-authored paraphrases and project prompts. The original framework guide is released under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
