Your free textbook reading plan

Short textbook readings support the same twelve-unit path. No book purchase, access code, programming background, or full-book sequence is required. Read the named scope of each listed source, then choose one idea or prompt for the project update you already submit.

Artificial Intelligence: Foundations of Computational Agents ↗
David L. Poole and Alan K. Mackworth. 3rd edition, Cambridge University Press, 2023. Primary academic AI foundation; only the listed conceptual passages are assigned. Free official author-hosted HTML, available with the publisher's permission.
Entrepreneurship ↗
Michael Laverty and Chris Littel. 1st edition, OpenStax, Rice University, 2020. Business companion for selected principles of needs, feasibility, resources, and adoption. Free official online textbook; no paid access code is required.
Introductory Statistics 2e ↗
Barbara Illowsky and Susan Dean. 2nd edition, OpenStax, Rice University, 2023. Quantitative companion for the specific statistics used in the course. Free official online textbook; no paid access code is required.

The AI text is an academic foundation; the course supplies its own current-tool guidance, no-code implementation activities, fictional cases, simulations, and assessment.

Optional articles and additional perspectives

The reading plan above is assigned. The following short articles are optional extensions. Original explanations, cases, figures and practice connect the textbooks to your usable solution.

Unit 1, 4, 11

The AI Fluency Framework — one-page guide ↗

Read the four competency definitions. Identify one decision about delegation, one instruction, one output check, and one responsibility in your task. The guide is one page; no account is required.

Unit 2

OpenStax — Descriptive Statistics introduction ↗

Read this short introduction to numerical and graphical descriptions. Explain why your choice of a summary should match the question you are investigating; the rest of the chapter is optional.

Unit 3, 7, 10, 12

Strategyzer — How to select the next best test ↗

Read the experiment-selection principles and the discovery/validation distinction. Name the uncertainty your next test addresses. The article’s suggested experiment cadence is not an added course requirement.

Unit 5, 9

IDEO.org Design Kit — Build & Run Prototypes ↗

Read the five short steps. Identify what your version helps you learn and how you will record feedback. For this capstone, the final prototype must also perform the bounded task; a mock-up alone is not the final deliverable.

Unit 6

OpenStax — Confidence Intervals introduction ↗

Read the opening explanation of point and interval estimates, stopping before the mathematical example. State why a sample estimate is uncertain. Derivations are optional.

Unit 8

Microsoft Learn — Understand cost drivers in generative AI ↗

Read this single cost-driver unit and identify an expense missing from your first estimate. Product examples illustrate the vendor’s perspective; buying or using those products is not required.
