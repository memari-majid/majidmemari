Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

A pilot is a learning plan

A useful pilot has a narrow business question, a defined user and task, a versioned usable solution, an access/delivery location, a time limit, and a decision it will inform. The Week 9 assignment is the readiness plan; the controlled deployment and user-test record are completed before the final capstone submissions. For example, a user can open a reviewed listing kit, complete a product-listing task, and explain what required correction. The pilot can occur privately in class with supplied fictional inputs. It must be a real test of the artifact, not invented results or only a proposal for future testing.

Measure the whole process

Include the time to prepare inputs, review outputs, correct mistakes, and handle exceptions. Compare the pilot with a baseline rather than with an imagined perfect manual process. Define a quality measure alongside speed or cost. Fictional numbers may illustrate the calculation, but they must be labeled assumptions. A plan is not a completed experiment; do not invent results.

Adoption requires people and ownership

A solution can be easy to demonstrate and difficult to adopt. Identify who does the work, who approves it, what training or guidance they need, and how concerns are handled. A small pilot should have a clear owner and a way to revert to the prior process. Staff feedback can reveal extra work or hidden dependencies that a polished concept misses. Draw a plain-language operating sequence: intake → AI-assisted preparation → human check → delivery → follow-up. For each handoff, name the owner, required evidence, and escalation condition. This operating playbook can be run manually with ordinary tools; an agent platform or integration is unnecessary.

Worked pilot protocol: plan the decision before the result

A bounded illustrative protocol is: “Compare both methods on the same 20 fictional product fact sheets. Alternate which method is used first to reduce simple practice effects. Record input preparation, drafting, checking, correction, and final factual accuracy. Keep the scope to product listings and have a designated reviewer approve each result.” A proposed decision rule might require at least 20% lower median total preparation time and no unsupported product claims in approved outputs. Those thresholds are classroom planning choices, not industry standards. Pause the exercise if review is bypassed or confidential information is introduced. A measurement log can have five columns: case, method, total minutes, factual corrections, and approval outcome. Leave results blank until work is actually performed. The Week 9 submission requires this plan; before the final capstone, execute the agreed smaller controlled user test and record its actual outcome. The illustrative 20-case comparison is a planning example, not a mandatory sample size. Never invent results to make a plan look complete.

Apply this lesson across business disciplines
Finance
Plan a small comparison of report accuracy, preparation time, and decision usefulness.
Marketing
Test the complete campaign-preparation or delivery process, including review and rework.
MBA / Strategy
Set a bounded pilot and define what evidence would justify the next investment.
MIS
Test a manual assistant workflow with supplied cases and explicit escalation thresholds.
Operations
Measure total cycle time and quality, not just a faster isolated task.

Carry this into your decision

Time saved in one step is not the same as time released by the complete process. Released capacity is not automatically a payroll saving or additional revenue.

Put the ideas to work

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Compare total time, mean time, and median time for the two methods.
  2. Does the scenario meet a proposed 20% total-time reduction threshold?
  3. How should the remaining unsupported claim affect a rule requiring none in approved output?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

Manual time totals 92 minutes and assisted time 80, a 12-minute or 12 ÷ 92 = 13.0% reduction. Means are 11.5 and 10 minutes. Medians are 11.5 and 9.5 minutes. The long assisted case affects the mean more than the median. The 20% reduction threshold is not met, and one approved output violates the zero-unsupported-claim quality rule. The appropriate conclusion is to revise or pause under those proposed rules, not to declare success from faster typical cases. Eight simulated rows also do not complete the proposed 20-case learning plan.

Workshop · create an artifact

Prepare the Pilot and Readiness Plan

  1. 1State one hypothesis for your chosen capstone opportunity and the decision it will inform.
  2. 2Define a controlled deployment using the semester’s permitted data and tools. Specify the usable version, access/delivery location, test-user role, and task; use the classroom sandbox if external use is unavailable.
  3. 3Specify the baseline, full-process effort, quality measure, review method, and proposed continue/revise/stop thresholds. Explain why the thresholds fit your business case.
  4. 4Assign responsibilities and describe staff guidance, escalation, and fallback. Add a compact intake–prepare–review–deliver–follow-up operating sequence with any bounded AI-assisted roles.
  5. 5Include the vendor/risk comparison and a short deployment log template. Leave outcome fields blank until the user test occurs, then reuse the completed record in the final capstone.
  6. 6Use this module’s data table to calculate and interpret the requested quantities. Keep counts, denominators, units, and uncertainty visible; incorporate the relevant table or small figure into the existing artifact rather than creating another submission.

Discussion · make your reasoning visible

What Would Make You Stop the Pilot?

Describe a small pilot for your capstone concept. State one success measure and one stop condition, then explain how the business would make the continue, revise, or stop decision. Write 150–250 words and respond to one classmate in 75–100 words with a specific question or improvement. Distinguish case facts, assumptions, and AI-generated suggestions; disclose any AI assistance. Use fictional or supplied case data. Use the instructor’s calendar for your live offering.

Suggested semester scoring: 5 points, with 3 for the original contribution and 2 for a constructive reply. Follow the arrangements designated for your offering. Independent learners can write a response and compare it with a peer.

Knowledge check · ungraded

Check your reasoning

A pilot saves five minutes per task but adds ten minutes of checking. What does this imply?
Business value depends on the complete workflow. Review, correction, training, and coordination can offset gains in a single step.
