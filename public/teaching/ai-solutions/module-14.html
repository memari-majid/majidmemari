What you will be able to do

  • Interpret the actual controlled user test without overstating market or financial results.
  • Revise the implemented product or service and reconcile its evidence, economics, and operating plan.
  • Prepare a usable handoff and a supported continue, revise, or stop recommendation.

Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

Check the complete business argument

A coherent recommendation connects the problem, proposed experience, evidence, economics, and adoption plan. Check that the customer and use case are consistent across documents. A proposal for a reviewed premium service should not use the costs of an unreviewed automated product. Make sure your measures assess the value you claim and that the pilot can realistically inform the decision. Also reconcile acquisition, selling, and delivery: an inexpensive offer cannot quietly depend on unlimited personal support, and a growth plan must fit capacity. Check that the sample artifact delivers the promised value and that a person remains accountable for every AI-assisted task.

Revise the reasoning before polishing the design

Visual polish helps communication, but unsupported demand claims, omitted costs, and unclear responsibilities are more consequential weaknesses. Rank revisions by their effect on the decision. Address factual gaps and contradictions first, then improve structure and readability. Reuse earlier material thoughtfully, removing claims that later evidence no longer supports.

Document learning and remaining limits

A strong final report shows what was implemented, how it was made available, what the test user actually did, what feedback or measurements were collected, and what changed. Fictional inputs are legitimate; invented test events are not. Distinguish a successful classroom task from proof of customer demand, revenue, or production reliability. A limited pilot can reveal that expansion should stop even when the minimum usable solution was completed. Provide the final artifact, short instructions, source/assumption record, known limitations, owner, and support/closeout plan. No continuing support commitment should be implied without agreement.

Worked consistency audit: claims must match the artifact

Use three columns: claim, supporting artifact or calculation, and needed revision. Example: “fully self-service” conflicts with the storyboard’s reviewer; revise to “reviewed service concept.” “Saves cash” conflicts with unchanged staffing; revise to “releases capacity under stated assumptions,” unless you can support an actual spending reduction. “Customers want this” relies only on supplied fictional responses; revise to “the classroom exercise identified assumptions to test.” “Lower cost” relies on drafting time alone; include review, correction, and ongoing support. These changes make the recommendation more credible without adding another project. Finish with a one-paragraph revision note: what changed, what evidence or inconsistency caused the change, and how the recommendation is now different. Preserve justified uncertainty rather than filling every gap with an invented number.

Fictional business case

Put the ideas to work

A capstone report claims a fully self-service product, but its storyboard shows a staff member reviewing every output. The economics omit that staff time. The pilot measures user satisfaction but the recommendation promises cost savings.

Discuss the decision

  1. Which inconsistencies would most affect the decision?
  2. How would you revise the proposal without overstating the concept’s maturity?
  3. Which measure should be added or which benefit claim should be narrowed?

Business numbers · interpret the evidence

Audit the evidence behind numerical claims

Fictional teaching data, not real customer, vendor, or pilot observations. A fictional review of 12 material claims in a capstone draft. “Supported” means traceable to supplied facts or a cited source for the stated claim; “assumption” means explicitly labeled; “unsupported” means neither. This is a writing audit, not a measure of product success.
Claim statusNumber of material claims
Supported7
Clearly labeled assumption3
Unsupported2
Total12

On a narrow screen, scroll the table sideways. Keyboard: focus the table and use the arrow keys.

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. What share of claims is supported and what share remains unsupported?
  2. If the two unsupported claims are removed, what is the supported share of the remaining claims?
  3. Why does the higher percentage not mean new evidence was collected?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

Supported claims are 7 ÷ 12 = 58.3%; unsupported claims are 2 ÷ 12 = 16.7%. Removing the unsupported claims leaves ten claims, of which 7 ÷ 10 = 70% are supported and 30% are labeled assumptions. The higher percentage comes from changing the denominator, not from collecting evidence. Removing an unnecessary unsupported claim can improve honesty, but a material uncertainty may instead need to remain visible as an assumption or open question. Report both counts and definitions so a percentage does not conceal what changed.

Workshop · create an artifact

Complete the strategy review

  1. 1Audit the report, deck, artifact, and figures for consistency across need, offer, acquisition/adoption, selling/approval, delivery, and economics. Use the worked claim–evidence–revision audit.
  2. 2Complete the agreed controlled user test if not already done. Record the artifact version, access check, user role, task, actual result, feedback, and any measured quality or time.
  3. 3Revise the artifact and calculations in response to the evidence. Keep supplied hypothetical data separate from actual classroom or partner test observations.
  4. 4Assemble the short handoff: final artifact/location, instructions, worked example, review checklist, known limitations, owner, and continue/revise/stop decision.
  5. 5Confirm that final submissions meet the existing assignment requirements and course deadlines.
  6. 6Use this module’s data table to calculate and interpret the requested quantities. Keep counts, denominators, units, and uncertainty visible; incorporate the relevant table or small figure into the existing artifact rather than creating another submission.
Your outputThe implemented capstone, completed deployment/user-test record, revised final-report draft, presentation deck, and concise handoff material; these feed the final assignments, not a new submission.

Discussion · make your reasoning visible

What Are You Revising and Why?

Identify the most consequential revision to your capstone after feedback or self-review. Explain the evidence or inconsistency that prompted it and how it changes the recommendation. Write 150–250 words and respond to one classmate in 75–100 words with a specific question or improvement. Distinguish case facts, assumptions, and AI-generated suggestions; disclose any AI assistance. Use fictional or supplied case data. Use the instructor’s calendar for your live offering.

Suggested semester scoring: 5 points, with 3 for the original contribution and 2 for a constructive reply. Follow the arrangements designated for your offering. Independent learners can write a response and compare it with a peer.
