Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

A portfolio makes tradeoffs visible

A business rarely has enough time, money, or attention to pursue every idea. Review your five case briefs as a portfolio. Compare expected customer or operational value, confidence in the evidence, effort, risks, and organizational fit. Use the same criteria for each opportunity so an attractive presentation does not substitute for a strong business case.

Scoring supports judgment

A simple one-to-five score can make reasoning explicit, but it is still a judgment based on limited information. Define what a high or low score means and explain the important scores. Do not hide uncertainty behind a precise total. An option with high potential value but weak evidence may need a small concept test before a larger investment.

Choose a manageable first step

The best initial opportunity is often narrow enough to evaluate and useful enough to matter. Identify dependencies such as staff time, content quality, approval, or customer acceptance. State a reason to proceed, defer, or reject each option. Select one opportunity for the remaining capstone assignments and explain what you would need to learn before expanding it. A one-person business coordinating AI-assisted research, drafting, review support, and customer follow-up is one possible operating scenario. It is not a forecast that all businesses will work this way or that staff effort disappears. Check which roles still consume the owner’s time and which tasks should remain manual before proposing expansion.

Worked prioritization: make the scoring inspectable

Suppose you use four criteria scored from 1 to 5, with higher always better: business value 30%, ease of a small pilot 25%, evidence confidence 25%, and manageability of risk 20%. These weights are an illustrative management choice. A product-listing concept scored 4, 4, 3, 4 totals 3.75: (4 × .30) + (4 × .25) + (3 × .25) + (4 × .20). A response-drafting concept scored 5, 3, 2, 2 totals 3.15. The numbers are judgments, not measurements. Explain why evidence confidence or risk scores differ. If later evidence raises the response concept’s confidence to 5 and risk manageability to 4, its score becomes 4.30 and the ranking changes. That reversal shows which information matters before a commitment. Use the matrix to organize a discussion; a small numerical lead is not an objective verdict.

Apply this lesson across business disciplines
Finance
Prioritize reporting improvements by decision value, effort, evidence, and review risk.
Marketing
Compare campaign or product opportunities without treating weighted scores as measured demand.
MBA / Strategy
Choose a first offering and a manageable operating scope before adding more AI-assisted roles.
MIS
Rank proposed information-service changes and check dependencies between them.
Operations
Choose a pilot that fits staff capacity and addresses a consequential bottleneck.

Put the ideas to work

Cedar Market is considering an AI-assisted product-description service, customer-response drafts, supplier-comparison summaries, a gift-finding concept, and a staff scheduling idea. The owner has limited review time. The scheduling idea could affect employees but has the weakest information and the most unresolved concerns.

Discuss the decision

  2. When should uncertainty outweigh an attractive estimated benefit?
  3. Which opportunity would be a sensible first pilot, and why?

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Calculate the weighted score of the listing kit and response drafts.
  2. Which opportunity ranks first under these assumptions?
  3. What happens if evidence raises response drafts’ confidence to 5 and risk manageability to 4?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

The listing kit scores 4×.30 + 4×.25 + 3×.25 + 4×.20 = 3.75. Response drafts score 3.15; supplier summaries 3.45; gift-finding 2.75; scheduling 2.45. The listing kit ranks first under these judgments. Raising the response option’s confidence and risk scores as stated produces 4.30 and changes the ranking. A small bar chart can communicate the original scores, but its caption must identify them as weighted judgments. The sensitivity result tells you which evidence could change the decision; it does not make either ranking objective.

Workshop · create an artifact

Assemble the AI Opportunity Portfolio

  1. 1Reuse your five case briefs and update any conclusion that changed after feedback.
  2. 2Define four or five comparison criteria and make score direction explicit. Use the worked matrix as a model, but explain the judgments for your own five opportunities.
  3. 3Create a one-page comparison matrix, explain material assumptions, and vary the most uncertain score or weight once to see whether the preferred option changes.
  4. 4Select one capstone opportunity and identify a practical first step.
  5. 5Add a short reflection on why two other ideas should be deferred or rejected.
  6. 6Use this module’s data table to calculate and interpret the requested quantities. Keep counts, denominators, units, and uncertainty visible; incorporate the relevant table or small figure into the existing artifact rather than creating another submission.

Discussion · make your reasoning visible

Which Opportunity Comes First?

Recommend one opportunity from your portfolio. Explain the criteria that drove your choice, one tradeoff you accepted, and the evidence that could change the ranking. Write 150–250 words and respond to one classmate in 75–100 words with a specific question or improvement. Distinguish case facts, assumptions, and AI-generated suggestions; disclose any AI assistance. Use fictional or supplied case data. Use the instructor’s calendar for your live offering.

Suggested semester scoring: 5 points, with 3 for the original contribution and 2 for a constructive reply. Follow the arrangements designated for your offering. Independent learners can write a response and compare it with a peer.

Knowledge check · ungraded

Check your reasoning

The listing concept scores 3.75. The response concept scores 3.15, but rises to 4.30 if evidence confidence and risk manageability improve. What does the reversal show?
The matrix makes a decision dependency visible: confidence and manageable risk can change the priority. The improved scores are a scenario, not new evidence. Investigate those assumptions and show both cases rather than treating either total as an objective verdict.
