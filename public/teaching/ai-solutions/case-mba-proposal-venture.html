Design an AI-Assisted Proposal Service

Service providers lose time turning incomplete client requests into understandable, consistent proposals. Faster text generation alone does not solve missing requirements, unrealistic promises, or delivery capacity.

Fictional supplied case · optional application

Understand the problem

EventQuote is a fictional small operator preparing reviewed proposal packs for independent event-service providers. The scenario explores how one person might coordinate AI-assisted research, drafting, checking, and follow-up. It is a possible operating design, not a prediction that autonomous agents will run a business successfully. This illustrative alternative uses the same milestones as the semester capstone. The confirmed brief and available resources determine the final scope. Complete a controlled partner or classroom deployment of the usable kit and record an actual user task.

Service providers lose time turning incomplete client requests into understandable, consistent proposals. Faster text generation alone does not solve missing requirements, unrealistic promises, or delivery capacity.

People involved

  • User: a provider preparing a proposal for its client.
  • Buyer: the provider’s owner or sales lead.
  • End reader: the client comparing scope, price, and next steps.
  • Accountable operator: the person approving the proposal pack.

Discover the need

Supplied evidence

  • Supplied fictional provider response: “The hardest part is clarifying what the client actually needs.”
  • Supplied fictional sales lead response: “A quick proposal is useless if it promises something our team cannot deliver.”
  • Supplied fictional prospective buyer response: “Show me one complete sample, your turnaround assumption, and what revisions are included.”
  • For the sample proposal, use a fictional workshop for 20 attendees, a two-hour session, a supplied $600 service quote, and an explicit exclusion of venue and catering. No supplier availability or booking is confirmed.

Questions to investigate

  • What requirement is commonly missing from an initial client request?
  • What makes the proposal useful to the provider and its client?
  • Which scope, timing, and revision boundaries would a buyer expect before paying?

Design the AI-assisted solution

  1. Collect the supplied requirements and list unresolved questions.
  2. Draft a proposal with scope, assumptions, exclusions, quote, and next action.
  3. Use a separate review pass to check every commitment against the requirements and supplied quote.
  4. Deliver the reviewed pack and a follow-up template; maintain an operator checklist for support and revisions.

The role of AI

Act as a requirements-organizing helper, proposal-drafting helper, and review-question helper. The operator may run these roles manually in a ready-made assistant; no autonomous agent system is required.

A reasonable non-AI alternative

A well-designed proposal template completed and reviewed by the operator.

Human review

The operator confirms facts and feasibility, approves prices and promises, and handles exceptions. No AI role may claim an actual booking or agreement that has not occurred.

Build and deliver an artifact

Proposal and Follow-Up Service Pack

A compact intake sheet, finished one- to two-page sample proposal, scope/claim check, and short follow-up message.

What the usable package contains

  • An intake sheet distinguishing known requirements from questions still needing a client answer.
  • A proposal for the supplied workshop including the $600 quote and explicit venue/catering exclusions.
  • A role-based operating checklist assigning preparation, verification, approval, and customer follow-up.
  • A hypothetical EventQuote service offer stating package price, included revisions, and the next sales step.

Acceptance checks

  • No venue, catering, date availability, or booking is invented.
  • The client can distinguish the underlying $600 event-service quote from EventQuote’s hypothetical proposal-preparation fee.
  • The pack handles a routine request, an incomplete request, and a request to promise an unconfirmed extra.
  • The operator can explain the time and responsibility required for each AI-assisted role.

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Calculate contribution after delivery and acquisition, then the remaining modeled value after the tool expense.
  2. Calculate acquisition cost per new customer and explain what is excluded.
  3. If the operator has eight hours weekly and reserves two for administration, how many 45-minute packs fit in the remaining capacity?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

Revenue is $720. Subtracting $150 delivery valuation and $90 acquisition leaves $480; subtracting the $60 tool expense leaves $420 before other excluded costs. This mixes staff-time valuation with cash expenses, so it is modeled economic value, not automatically cash profit. Acquisition cost is $90/6=$15 per new customer. Six available weekly production hours equal 360 minutes, allowing eight 45-minute packs before additional disruptions or support. The six fictional monthly orders do not establish demand, repeat purchase, or a reliable capacity forecast.

Run a bounded pilot

Does the complete pack solve a useful proposal-preparation problem with accurate commitments and a manageable delivery workload?

Plan

  • Build the finished pack and assign its bounded AI-assisted and human roles.
  • Test the routine workshop case, an inquiry with no attendee count, and a request to include unconfirmed catering.
  • Record errors, missing information, full preparation/review time if actually timed, and one revision.
  • Role-play a buyer who asks for unlimited revisions or guaranteed conversion; respond with an honest scope and next step.
  • Make the versioned kit available to an approved partner or classroom user, verify access, record a defined task and actual feedback, revise, and provide a short handoff. This is the required controlled deployment; no public release is necessary.

Success measures

  • The proposal preserves the supplied facts and exclusions in all three tests.
  • Missing information is surfaced instead of invented.
  • The hypothetical offer and operator workload fit the same scope and revision assumptions.
  • A partner or classroom recipient can access the artifact and complete the agreed task; the record accurately describes the outcome, including failures.

When to pause or stop

  • The pack invents availability, bookings, commitments, or a guarantee that a proposal will win business.
  • Review and support effort exceed capacity or make the modeled economics untenable.

Make the adoption or business decision

Value inside an organization

A proposal-preparation improvement inside an existing business can be evaluated without selling a new service.

A possible venture

A possible one-person, AI-assisted proposal service with a clear offer, first-customer channel, role-play sales process, delivery checklist, and improvement plan. No actual customer recruitment, contract, payment, or public launch is required.

Assumptions to test

  • Whether providers consider proposal preparation worth paying for.
  • Scope and frequency of revisions, support, and incomplete requests.
  • Actual customer acquisition cost and repeat-order behavior.
  • The operator’s capacity and the quality of AI-assisted work under review.
