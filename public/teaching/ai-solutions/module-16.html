Seminar · understand the decision

Ideas to work with

Transfer the decision process

The course’s core process applies beyond any particular AI tool: define a problem, compare alternatives, examine evidence, make the concept understandable, estimate value, and plan responsible adoption. Tools will change, but the need for clear judgment remains. Review your work for the parts of that process that became stronger.

Choose a proportionate next step

A completed capstone includes an implemented business artifact or service and a controlled user test; it is not automatically a validated market or a production-ready application. A next step may be an appropriately approved partner pilot, further customer discovery, improved reliability, or closing the classroom project. Match the next step to the uncertainty and resources that matter. This optional reflection adds no new requirement.

Keep an honest record of the work

If you use the project in a portfolio, label fictional data, classroom role-play, illustrative artifacts, and AI assistance. Describe your own analysis and decisions clearly. A concise account of what you learned and changed is more credible than claiming a launched business or implemented system that does not exist.

An accurate portfolio description

An accurate portfolio description could be: “Implemented a reviewed retail-listing kit, delivered a version to a classroom test user, documented a task using fictional product data, and revised the kit from the feedback. Produced scenario economics and a handoff plan.” Describe the actual delivery method, role of AI, your own contribution, and limits. Do not turn a private classroom deployment into a claim of commercial launch, real customer validation, or realized savings. The optional reflection creates no new graded work, meeting, submission, or deadline.

Business numbers · interpret the evidence

Transfer the method, then recheck the denominator

Fictional teaching data, not real customer, vendor, or pilot observations. Optional arithmetic reflection only. Compare two fictional contexts with the same four-minute capacity gain per task, $24 hourly staff-time valuation, and $60 monthly tool expense. Payroll is unchanged in both.

On a narrow screen, scroll the table sideways. Keyboard: focus the table and use the arrow keys.

Read the numbers before deciding

  1. Why does the recommendation change even though the per-task improvement is the same?
  2. What is the recurring volume threshold for capacity value to equal the $60 expense?
  3. Which assumptions need checking before transferring a claim to another business?
Compare your reasoning with the worked answer

At 20 tasks, 20×4÷60×$24 = $32 of assumed capacity value, below the $60 expense. At 100 tasks it is $160. Each task releases capacity valued at $1.60, so the threshold is $60÷$1.60 = 37.5 tasks; 38 whole tasks exceed it under the assumptions. This is not a cash break-even claim because payroll remains unchanged. Recheck volume, actual time saved, review quality, staff use of the released capacity, and costs in the new context. This optional table adds no submission, grade, meeting, or deadline.
