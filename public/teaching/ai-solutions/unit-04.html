Design the workflow

How does an input become a useful, checked output?

Learn

By the end of this unit

  • Assign each workflow step an input, output, and accountable person.
  • Specify a bounded AI role, source check, exception, and fallback.

Make the handoffs visible

A workflow explains how work moves, including the awkward cases. Cedar Desk’s ordinary task begins with a question and ends with a checked draft or an owner-ready exception note. The AI step sits inside that process. The workflow must still function when a source is missing, the assistant is unavailable, or the customer asks for an exception.

Grounding means connecting the answer to the information that is allowed to support it. Here, retrieval is simply finding S2 or S3 in a document. You do not need a technical retrieval system to understand the business requirement. An answer citing S2 can still misread its boundary, so a citation is a starting point for verification, not proof of correctness.

A fixed workflow says what happens next under a known condition. An agent could choose whether to retrieve another source, request clarification, or draft. That flexibility also creates a need for boundaries. A role brief should name its goal, allowed inputs, permitted actions, required output, human review, and stop condition. “Handle customer support” is too broad. “Prepare a draft from S1–S6; hold exceptions for the owner” is inspectable.

Choose a tool against the job

List requirements before comparing products: source visibility, acceptable information handling, output export, reviewer access, cost within the offering’s budget, and a usable manual fallback. Run the same fictional cases through any candidate. Mark an advertised feature separately from observed behavior. A free or familiar tool is not automatically suitable, and an unavailable paid feature should not be necessary to finish the course.

Trace a case that the workflow must stop

A participant cancels 8 hours before a workshop because of an emergency. The AI proposes: “We have issued a full refund.” Predict where the workflow should interrupt this draft.

Worked handoff

S2 supplies no automatic refund or credit under 24 hours. The reviewer holds the draft and prepares: “The request is 8 hours before the session. The participant reports an emergency. Policy requires owner review. Decision needed: whether an exception is authorized.” A customer-facing draft can acknowledge the request and explain that the owner must review it, without promising the outcome or a response time absent from the source.

“Escalated” should mean a real handoff in a live service. In a classroom simulation, record “prepared owner handoff” unless a person actually received it. Do not mark a decision completed because the assistant wrote a plausible sentence.

Remove access to the AI tool. A coordinator can still use the source card and response template. Remove the current policy instead: drafting must pause. The source is an essential dependency; the tool is replaceable.

Turn the charter into an operating design

Add a five-to-seven-step workflow to your project record. For each step, name the input, useful output, responsible person, and condition that moves or stops the work. Use an ordered list or simple boxes; arrows alone do not assign responsibility.

Write a role brief for the AI-assisted drafting step. Attach one ordinary example and one exception. Define a material error for this project: a claim or action that could change the customer’s eligibility, price, booking status, or expected commitment without source support. A grammar preference is a different kind of defect.

Finally, specify the manual fallback and how the reviewer sees the source. Check that an owner-ready exception still completes the intended task, even though the original customer request remains unresolved. The next unit turns these instructions into files a second person can actually use.
