iExplain

Make the handoffs visible

A workflow explains how work moves, including the awkward cases. Cedar Desk’s ordinary task begins with a question and ends with a checked draft or an owner-ready exception note. The AI step sits inside that process. The workflow must still function when a source is missing, the assistant is unavailable, or the customer asks for an exception.

Grounding means connecting the answer to the information that is allowed to support it. Here, retrieval is simply finding S2 or S3 in a document. You do not need a technical retrieval system to understand the task requirement. An answer citing S2 can still misread its boundary, so a citation is a starting point for verification, not proof of correctness.

A fixed workflow says what happens next under a known condition. An agent could choose whether to retrieve another source, request clarification, or draft. That flexibility also creates a need for boundaries. A role brief should name its goal, allowed inputs, permitted actions, required output, human review, and stop condition. “Handle customer support” is too broad. “Prepare a draft from S1–S6; hold exceptions for the owner” is inspectable.

Choose a tool against the job

List requirements before comparing products: source visibility, acceptable information handling, output export, reviewer access, cost within the offering’s budget, and a usable manual fallback. Run the same fictional cases through any candidate. Mark an advertised feature separately from observed behavior. A free or familiar tool is not automatically suitable, and an unavailable paid feature should not be necessary to finish the course.

Read the short guide here

Four habits for working with AI. This original course explanation applies the AI Fluency Framework to your project; it is not a reproduction of the publisher's one-page guide.

Delegation — choose the work
Decide whether AI is useful for this task, which step it may assist, and which decisions a person must retain.
Description — make the task clear
Give the permitted inputs, goal, audience, output format, constraints, and a clear stopping rule.
Discernment — inspect the result
Compare the output with sources and success criteria. Check for missing conditions, invented claims, and whether it helps complete the intended task.
Diligence — remain responsible
Own the decision to use or release the result. Protect information, disclose material AI assistance, and arrange human review or escalation where needed.

Decide what the workflow may release

A participant cancels 8 hours before a workshop because of an emergency. The supplied AI draft promises an automatic full refund. Inspect the approved source, then choose whether to release the draft, revise it, or prepare an owner handoff. A customer-facing sentence and an authorized action are different things.

=Worked reasoning

One emergency, two review policies

The fixed challenge uses the same fictional 8-hour emergency throughout. S2 supplies no automatic refund or credit under 24 hours; an owner reviews exceptional requests. S6 keeps commitments with an authorized person. No refund, delivery, or owner decision has actually happened in this teaching exercise.

PolicySource and authority checkCustomer consequenceUnsupported commitments released in this example

Check the source and route the exceptionReviewer holds the unsupported promise; owner must decide.A bounded reply acknowledges the request and explains the required review.0

The difference comes from the review and handoff rule, not a different customer or a more confident draft. On the ordinary 96-hour cancellation, S2 establishes refund eligibility, but a coordinator must still process the request. Even a source-consistent draft does not establish that a booking changed or money was returned.

Prepare an honest handoff

A useful owner note is: “The request is 8 hours before the session. The participant reports an emergency. Policy requires owner review. Decision needed: whether an exception is authorized.” A customer-facing draft can acknowledge the request and explain that the owner must review it, without promising the outcome or a response time absent from the source.

“Escalated” should mean a real handoff in a live service. In this classroom simulation, record “prepared owner handoff” unless a person actually received it. Do not mark a decision completed because the assistant wrote a plausible sentence. The counts above describe this one supplied case and do not prove that the workflow prevents every future error.

Remove access to the AI tool: a coordinator can still use the source card and response template. Remove the current policy instead: drafting must pause. The source is an essential dependency; the tool is replaceable.

Carry forward: name the source owner, reviewer, permitted action, exception route, stop condition, and manual fallback. Record what the workflow actually completed, separately from the unresolved customer request.

Goal: Write a short workflow, observe its consequences, and revise where source checking, human authority and communication occur. Your role: Cedar Desk workflow designer.

You are helping Cedar Desk rehearse a reply workflow. Policies and requests below are fictional teaching inputs. Run the same workflow on A and B, revise it once, then try the frozen second workflow on C. No real customer, owner, booking, refund or credit is affected.

Read the approved source card

  • S1 — Session: A Standard Workshop lasts 90 minutes and costs $40 per participant, including materials. Participants must be at least 16.
  • S2 — Cancellation: At least 72 hours before the session: refund eligibility. From 24 hours up to, but not including, 72 hours: credit valid for 90 days. Under 24 hours: no automatic refund or credit; the owner reviews exceptional requests. Do not promise a payment-processing date.
  • S3 — Rescheduling: A request made at least 48 hours before the session may be considered, subject to available space and coordinator confirmation. No availability is supplied in this packet.
  • S4 — Missing facts: Ask only for the missing fact needed to apply a policy. Classroom booking references are fictional. A draft is not confirmation that a booking was changed or a refund was processed.
  • S5 — Special requests: Group prices, accessibility arrangements, age exceptions, and policy exceptions require owner review. The packet supplies no basis for promising them.
  • S6 — Release: A person checks the source, conditions, tone, and next step before a reply leaves the service. A human decides exceptions and authorizes commitments.

Your shift board

  1. Request A · 9:05

    Can I move my booking to a later session? My class is in about 60 hours.

  2. Request B · 10:00 — 8 hours before the session

    I have a family emergency and can't make today's 6 p.m. session. Can I get my money back?

  3. Request C · Session time not supplied

    Please cancel my booking.

Run → revise → try a new request

  1. Write up to six workflow steps (1,200 characters). Before looking at the worked route, trace the same steps on A and B: what is drafted, what does a person check, what is sent, and what reaches the owner?
  2. Write a short revision reason (up to 200 characters). Revise once and trace A and B again.
  3. Freeze the second workflow. Trace C without adding or changing a step. Record which missing fact changes the decision.

In the interactive route, AI only classifies quoted actions in your steps. You can classify them yourself. Fixed code selects the authored consequences; AI does not invent a customer reaction or judge a grade.

Compare with the worked shift

Workflow v1

  1. Read the message.
  2. Find the matching policy on the source card.
  3. Ask the AI to write a friendly reply based on that policy.
  4. Check tone and accuracy.
  5. Send.

Workflow v2

  1. Read the message.
  2. Find the matching approved policy.
  3. Ask AI to draft the policy’s conditions and never claim a booking changed.
  4. Check tone, accuracy, commitments and confirmations; hold unapproved refund, credit, seat or exception promises.
  5. Route held messages to the owner.
  6. Send checked replies only if not held.

Revision reason: Both mistakes were promises nobody approved.

Turn the charter into an operating design

Add a five-to-seven-step workflow to your project record. For each step, name the input, useful output, responsible person, and condition that moves or stops the work. Use an ordered list or simple boxes; arrows alone do not assign responsibility.

Write a role brief for your chosen AI capability: permitted input, instruction or operation, expected output, source or reference check, and stop condition. An extraction project needs a field schema and missing-value rule; an image or speech project needs a permitted input, usable format, and a check of meaning or required details; a prediction needs a baseline and a planned comparison with observations. Explain whether you will operate an available tool or simulate the AI step using labeled supplied outputs.

Attach one ordinary example and one exception. Define a material error for your task: an error that changes the usefulness, meaning, eligibility, or authorized action of the result. In ReplyKit this includes unsupported claims about price, booking status, or commitments. A grammar preference is a different kind of defect.

Finally, specify the manual fallback and how the reviewer sees the source. Check that an owner-ready exception still completes the intended task, even though the original customer request remains unresolved. The next unit turns these instructions into files a second person can actually use.
