# Academic source and accuracy policy

Owner: Majid Memari. Effective 11 November 2026.

The course uses established academic textbooks for AI, business/implementation, and quantitative concepts. A publisher's reputation does not make every statement infallible. Support a teaching claim with the relevant section and its actual content, not merely a book title.

## Source and claim checks

1. Record author, exact edition, publisher, section title/number, official URL, inspected passage boundary, and date checked. Use stable section locators rather than invented page numbers or an unverified edition.
2. Distinguish established concepts, a course-authored method or interpretation, a fictional worked example, a planning assumption, and an actual observation. Book support for a principle is not empirical validation of the course's fictional numbers or a learner's solution.
3. Inspect the assigned passage. Exclude advanced derivations, algorithms, programming exercises, and historical legal/product claims that do not fit a beginner course. Explain the bridge from the passage to the project in original plain language.
4. Check current technical or empirical claims against primary research, standards, or official technical documentation when the textbook does not establish them. Qualify uncertainty; correct or remove unsupported factual claims.
5. Recalculate worked numbers independently. Keep units, denominators, observation windows, quality constraints, cash versus valued effort, and supplied versus hypothetical states consistent across prose, tables, figures, feedback, and rubrics.
6. Ask an independent reviewer to flag false statements, overclaims, unsupported generalization, and missing assumptions. Record the review scope and limitations. Technical tests and source checks do not establish that all possible errors have been eliminated or that learning effectiveness has been measured.
7. Recheck sources and current tool instructions before each offering. Use the publisher's errata process when appropriate; do not present dated law, prices, tools, or forecasts as current guarantees.

## Assigned reading and feedback

The required reading plan uses free official textbook pages. Each unit identifies exactly what to read, what to skip, why it matters, and one application to the continuing project. The existing zero-point written project update records a brief book/section citation and an applied idea. It does not add a new assignment, grading group, or completion requirement.

Readings appear within the lesson. If the source reader fails, use the original course connection and worked example, record the access problem, and ask through Canvas for support. This access alternative is labeled accurately; using it is not a claim that the external textbook was read. Reading estimates are provisional, not measured credit-hour equivalents.

## Content ownership and the course TA

Official textbook content remains on the author/publisher servers. The course stores bibliographic metadata, original assignment directions, original explanations, and original questions. It does not package book chapters, publisher figures, restricted educator assets, or a downloaded book-text corpus.

The course TA receives the public course explanations and assigned-reading metadata. It does not receive the complete linked books and must not invent quotations, page numbers, or claims that it inspected an unavailable chapter. A source citation should distinguish the book's stated principle from the course's application of it. Canvas remains authoritative for learner submissions, instructor feedback and grades.

The [textbook plan](textbook-recommendation.md) and the section-level claim/source review identify the selected materials and their limits. Academic sources support the teaching; no textbook publisher or university is represented as accrediting or endorsing this independent course.
