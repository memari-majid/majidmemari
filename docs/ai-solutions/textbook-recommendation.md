# Required textbook foundation and reading plan

Owner: Majid Memari. Adopted for the current design on **11 November 2026**, following the request for both AI and business textbook support and a free reading route.

The course assigns selected passages from three established academic textbooks. All readings are free on the official author/publisher websites. No purchased book, access code, platform subscription, or full-book prerequisite is required. The exact student assignments, stopping points, original course connections and application questions are maintained in [site/textbook_readings.py](course-source/textbook_readings.py), displayed in every lesson, and listed in the [reading guide](https://www.majidmemari.com/teaching/ai-solutions/readings.html).

## AI foundation

**David L. Poole and Alan K. Mackworth, _Artificial Intelligence: Foundations of Computational Agents_, third edition, Cambridge University Press, 2023.** Hardback ISBN **9781009258197**.

The [publisher's front matter](https://assets.cambridge.org/97810092/58197/frontmatter/9781009258197_frontmatter.pdf) identifies the edition and academic scope. The [official complete HTML text](https://artint.info/3e/html/ArtInt3e.html) is available free with Cambridge's permission. Its [preface](https://artint.info/3e/html/ArtInt3e.Chx1.html) describes its intended advanced undergraduate/graduate audience and technical content. This course assigns only inspected conceptual passages about tasks, agents, success criteria, people, evaluation and responsibility. Algorithms, mathematical derivations, Python and textbook exercises are not assigned.

The book supports foundational AI concepts. It does not establish that a particular current AI tool is accurate, universally available, or authorized to act. Current generative-AI limitations and evaluation guidance also use inspected primary material such as [NIST AI 600-1](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf).

## Business and implementation foundation

**Michael Laverty and Chris Littel, _Entrepreneurship_, OpenStax, 2020.**

The [official preface](https://openstax.org/books/entrepreneurship/pages/preface) identifies the authors, publication date, Rice University/OpenStax context, introductory undergraduate scope, free access and review process. Selected passages support need research, feasible scope, resources, cash-flow distinctions, understanding users and communicating a useful next request.

These principles apply to personal, community, research, organizational and commercial solutions. The original course connections explicitly translate the business examples into the learner's context. Starting a business, seeking investment, making a sale or launching publicly is not required. Commercialization remains an optional application; textbook language does not change the universal capstone criteria.

## Quantitative companion

**Barbara Illowsky and Susan Dean, _Introductory Statistics 2e_, OpenStax, 2023.**

The [official preface](https://openstax.org/books/introductory-statistics-2e/pages/preface) documents its authors, review process, free access and full-book prerequisites. The course uses short inspected explanations of averages, experimental design and misleading comparisons; it does not assign the complete statistics sequence or import its intermediate-algebra prerequisite.

## How readings become course work

Each unit provides a short required selection, exact reading/stopping instructions, a reason for reading it, an original plain-language connection, and an application question. The plan contains **189 minutes** of provisional reading/application time across twelve units, approximately **12–20 minutes per unit**. These estimates are design assumptions, not measured completion times.

Students include **one brief applied idea with the book and section citation** in the existing zero-point Canvas project update. There is no extra reading assignment, new grading group, or new progress gate. Reading supports the ongoing project and the existing evidence criteria. The current provisional estimates include the new reading time: roughly **77–135 minutes for guided units** and **40–67 hours total**, including additional project/review work. The instructor should calibrate these estimates with learners.

The required pages are displayed in the lesson reader; content remains on the official source server. If a source cannot load, the learner uses the original course connection and worked example, records the access issue, and completes the same reasoning in Canvas. This is an explicit access alternative, not a claim to have read an unavailable book passage. Other articles, method guides, professional books and unassigned book sections remain optional.

## Accuracy and source boundaries

The [academic source policy](academic-source-policy.md) requires section-level support, current primary evidence where needed, and independent numerical checks. The [claim/source map](claim-source-map.md) distinguishes supported concepts, original teaching procedures, fictional inputs and conditional calculations. The simulations' invented data and dollar rates are not textbook empirical findings.

A respected publisher and peer review support source quality; they do not make a text infallible or constitute accreditation/endorsement of this independent course. The review inspected assigned passages and the current course content, not every page of each book. Historical legal and tool claims outside the assigned scope are not current course guidance.

Book chapters, publisher figures, restricted instructor resources and downloaded book text are not copied into the portable course or the AI TA corpus. The TA receives course-authored explanations, reading directions and bibliographic metadata; it must not invent quotations or page references. See the source policy for maintenance and licensing boundaries.

The [earlier research catalog](textbook-selection.md) is preserved as historical comparison. Its professional and entrepreneurship recommendations are not the required student reading list.
