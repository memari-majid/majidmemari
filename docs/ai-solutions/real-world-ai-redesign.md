# Real-world AI capability and implementation redesign

Design decision: 11 November 2026. This extends the current instructional plan in response to Majid Memari's direction that the course serve everyone, with no prerequisites, and teach how to build AI solutions for real-world problems.

## Learning spine

One path, twelve units, one continuing project, five milestones. Keep Learn → Try → Build → Finish, the approved milestone weights, Spring 2027 dates, Canvas grading and progress, embedded simulations and course TA.

Every project must connect a need to an AI capability, usable inputs and output, a practical no-code implementation, a human check, observed use, revision, and responsible handoff. More automation is not automatically a better solution.

## Capability literacy and application

Unit 1 teaches an explicit map: generate/transform text; retrieve answers from sources; extract/classify; analyze/predict from data; interpret or create images; transcribe/translate/speak; coordinate actions through workflows and agents. Examples cover personal, community, creative, research and organizational needs. Describe tool availability and errors accurately: the capability depends on the selected tool and permitted inputs. Model output is a candidate, not verified evidence or an authorized action.

Learners compare capabilities against a concrete task, inspect supplied outputs without needing an account, choose one relevant capability for their project, and test its specific failure modes. Units 3–5 turn the choice into a bounded workflow and an operable artifact. Units 6–7 test and revise it; Units 8–12 evaluate impact/resources, prepare access, support adoption, operate and hand off. A supplied-output route supports participation but must be labeled as a simulated AI step rather than a live model integration.

## Assessment and later-unit correction

The audit found required commercial constructs in Units 9–10 and Milestone 4. Replace required price/contribution/sales/acquisition work with preparation for real use and supported adoption. Preserve quantitative reasoning and distinguish valued time from cash.

Milestone 4 becomes **Impact and adoption plan**. Its internal weights stay 35/20/25/20: impact/resources/capacity; uncertainty and decision threshold; readiness and route to users; onboarding and feedback. Commercialization is an optional application and is not required for full credit.

Use “user and responsible decision owner” in the required core; the learner can be that owner for an appropriate personal project. Replace executive/business-condition requirements with clear evidence and changed real-world conditions. Keep the fictional Cedar Desk continuity case and add transfer examples rather than require a second project.

## Numerical and implementation contracts

Preserve the twelve-case manual/unchecked/reviewed benchmark, rework boundary, monthly adoption scenarios, capacity-versus-cash distinction, review queue and chart-origin example. Reframe Unit 9's 180−108−12=60 as resource-value allowance (not cash remaining or profit). Reframe Unit 10's 270÷3=90 as resource cost per observed successful first-task completion; distinguish reach, conversation, agreement and completed use. Align accessible tables, figures, quizzes and feedback with these meanings.

Record source intent separately from live verification. This design plan precedes implementation; effectiveness requires learner evidence beyond technical tests.
