Find a real need, design a useful solution, build it with AI and no-code tools, test it, and put it into use. No prior AI, programming, or business background is required.

One path, twelve units, one project

  1. Learn and try inside Canvas. Each lesson follows Learn → Try → Build → Finish. Interactive figures, simulations, and the course AI TA are embedded in the lesson.
  2. Use Canvas Next to submit your unit check-in. Record your reasoning, calculations, or evidence. These short check-ins receive formative feedback and record submission progress.
  3. Build one capstone and submit five snapshots. Use an approved real-world problem or the complete fictional Cedar Desk case. Continue through Canvas Modules in order.

Unit check-ins are zero-point Complete/Incomplete activities for progress and feedback. The five milestones determine the final grade. Read returned comments, rubric scores, and grades in Canvas Grades. Assignment dates are set for the offering.

Questions and feedback

  • Course questions and project exchange: ask general questions and offer constructive peer feedback. This discussion is optional and ungraded.
  • Canvas Inbox: private questions about assessment, access, or individual circumstances.
  • Course AI TA: use the assistant inside any lesson for explanations and hints. It uses public course material and cannot inspect your Canvas submissions or grades.

What counts as progress? Canvas records lesson views and required submissions. A submitted check-in records receipt, not correctness or mastery. Simulation controls are practice; submit your answers and evidence in Canvas. Instructor assessment and feedback stay in Canvas.
