Course syllabus · Asynchronous delivery

Building AI Solutions

Instructor: Majid Memari

Discover a real-world need, build and test an AI-assisted product or service, evaluate its value, find users, and deliver a responsible handoff. This course is open to everyone, from any discipline. There are no prerequisites: no prior AI, programming, statistics, or business background is required.

Asynchronous course path

Follow Units 01–12 in Canvas Modules. Each lesson uses Learn → Try → Build → Finish, with original core reading, a text/table alternative, embedded simulations, and the course TA. Use Canvas Next for an exchange when listed, then the native quiz/project update, then a milestone when due. The Unit 12 milestone is followed by the individual case exam and completion checklist. No live attendance is required for this route.

Work on one continuing capstone using an approved need or the complete fictional Cedar Desk case. A usable document-based kit or repeatable service is sufficient; a mock-up alone is not the final deliverable. No business partner, paid AI account, sale, public deployment, or payment is required. External work must stay within approved scope and information permissions.

Use an ordinary document tool and a calculator or spreadsheet. Required original reading and supplied outputs are available inside Canvas. External references and books are optional. Allow approximately 65–115 minutes per guided unit plus project work; 37–63 total learner hours is a provisional design estimate, not measured effort or academic-credit equivalence. Actual offering dates and requirements appear in Canvas.

What you will learn

  • Identify a specific need, user, accountable decision owner, and measurable success criteria.
  • Use AI or critically evaluate supplied outputs while checking sources, calculations, authority, and missing facts.
  • Build a bounded usable solution, observe another person using it, revise, and retest.
  • Interpret statistics, tables, figures, denominators, uncertainty, complete effort, costs, and capacity without programming.
  • Develop a truthful offer or internal adoption case and communicate a proportionate next decision.
  • Prepare operating instructions, human review, exceptions, maintenance, support limits, and handoff.

Twelve native quizzes contain three one-point choice questions and one zero-point project-update essay. Their technical raw scores are formative, omitted from the final grade, and in a 0%-weight group. They replace the earlier unit check-ins. Three project exchanges add formative critique with a supplied-sample alternative; the general course-questions forum is optional. No practice score or forum score contributes to the final grade.

Milestones 1–4 each use a 100-point rubric. Milestone 5 consists of a 60-point shared demonstration/handoff and a 40-point individual case exam in the same 25% group, contributing 15 and 10 percentage points of the whole course. The exam evaluates two 20-point criteria: quantitative understanding, and design judgment/ownership. Earlier individual explanations are preparation, not an additional numerical grade.

Submit a dated snapshot of the same record as files, concise text, or a reviewer-accessible artifact link according to the assignment. Include access instructions, source/AI-use disclosure, and honest pending items. Use the posted dates and revision windows. A written alternative can replace a recording or presentation. A justified revision, narrowing, or stop decision can demonstrate learning; a successful sale or favorable AI result is not a grading condition.

Canvas is the official submission and feedback record. A lesson view or submission requirement tracks a visit or receipt, not correctness or mastery. Interactive practice, exports, local browser state, and TA conversation are not submitted automatically. The instructor grades the milestones and individual exam using the rubric; the essay and exam are not automatically assessed by AI.

Read feedback, revise, and keep moving

  1. After submitting the unit quiz, open its results and read the explanation for each choice. Your project-update essay is a separate zero-point response: it is not automatically evaluated by AI and can remain pending manual review.
  2. For a milestone or the individual exam, open the assignment from Canvas Grades. Read the instructor comments and the criterion-level rubric before revising. A submission receipt or a raw practice score does not establish mastery.
  3. Use the feedback to change the same artifact or reasoning. Keep the previous version, add a short “feedback → change → evidence” note, and resubmit through the assignment when its revision window is open. For the individual exam, use the announced initial-submission and revision opportunity; the final reviewed submission counts.
  4. If an item is incomplete or feedback is unclear, name the unit, criterion, what you tried, and the specific question in Canvas Inbox. Do not create a new project to replace an unresolved issue.

Facilitated-course planning targets: a response to a course/private question within two working days, and milestone or exam feedback within five working days, while the facilitated offering is active. The instructor must confirm the working-day calendar, availability, and revision windows before enrollment. These are targets, not instant or round-the-clock service; self-study does not include promised instructor review.

While waiting, complete the next original reading, practice with the classroom case, check sources, or improve a non-consequential part of your record. Do not treat silence, a quiz score, or TA advice as approval to use private data, deploy externally, contact customers, spend money, or make commitments. If a confirmed response window passes, send one concise follow-up through Canvas Inbox and continue with the safe classroom route.

Arrange an actual user attempt without needing a partner

Use the complete fictional Cedar Desk case or an approved real-world need. No fee, business partner, paid AI subscription, public launch, or sale is required. A document-based kit or repeatable service can be a usable solution.

For the access check, pilot, and retest, ask another person to attempt a bounded task with your version. A classmate, colleague, friend, or assigned classroom reviewer can work asynchronously using the instructions you supply. Record what actually happened, including assistance, missing measurements, and unresolved problems. The case packet supplies facts and examples; it does not supply your observation.

If no reviewer is available, request a bounded classroom review through Canvas Inbox early enough for the published feedback window. Include your version, one safe task, expected behavior, and the particular observation you need. The instructor arranges an appropriate review/retest route while the facilitated course is active; do not assume instant access. Self-review and AI role-play help preparation, but neither replaces another person's actual attempt. Keep the requirement pending until it occurs.

Use AI openly and keep your own reasoning

Course materials, a calculator, and disclosed AI assistance are allowed, including on the individual case exam. You must author and explain your own analysis, identify the tool or supplied output, show a representative prompt where applicable, and record an important claim or calculation you checked and corrected. A fully generated answer without your verified reasoning does not meet the evidence criteria.

The course TA explains public course material and offers hints. It cannot inspect Canvas submissions, grades, private feedback, or your saved project, and it does not grade the essay, milestones, or exam. Instructor assessment uses the published criteria. Label supplied facts, actual observations, assumptions, classroom role-play, and AI-generated material accurately. Never invent a user test, customer, source, result, or payment.

Receiving-offering policies

Retained offering policies below apply alongside the asynchronous course arrangements. Late-work rules apply when an offering posts an assignment due date. Ask through Canvas Inbox for clarification or access support.

Late Work

Assignments are accepted up to 3 days late with a 10% penalty per day. After 3 days, no credit is given without prior arrangement. Contact the instructor before the deadline if you need an extension.

AI Use and Evidence

Use fictional or public non-sensitive information. Label case assumptions, simulated feedback, and AI-generated material. Verify important claims with credible sources. AI-generated customer personas and simulated interviews are practice material, not evidence of real customer demand. If access to an AI tool is unavailable, use the supplied sample output or complete the same analysis manually.

  • Disclose the AI tool used, its purpose, one representative prompt, and how you checked and changed its output.
  • You are responsible for every claim, calculation, citation, and recommendation in your submission.
  • Do not present fictional or simulated interviews, customers, performance, or financial results as real. Do not fabricate citations.
  • Grading rewards business judgment, evidence, and clear communication; polished AI output alone is insufficient.

Academic Integrity

Submit your own analysis, identify collaborators where applicable, and cite sources and AI assistance. Do not copy another student’s work or present invented evidence as real. See the UVU Academic Integrity Policy.

Accessibility

Students who need accommodations should contact UVU Accessibility Services and provide accommodation letters to the instructor as early as possible.
