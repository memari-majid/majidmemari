Building AI Solutions

Instructor: Majid Memari

Discover a real-world need, build and test an AI-assisted product or service, evaluate its value, find users, and deliver it responsibly. Students from any discipline use statistics, tables, figures, and ready-made AI or no-code tools. No prerequisites are required: no prior AI, programming, or business background is needed.

How to take this course in Canvas

Follow Units 01–12 in Canvas Modules. Each lesson contains Learn → Try → Build → Finish, with interactive simulations and the course AI TA embedded in Canvas. Use Canvas’s Next button to continue to the unit check-in and, when shown, the milestone submission.

Canvas is the official record. Submit your reasoning, calculations, and evidence to the unit check-ins and capstone assignments. Canvas records submissions and instructor feedback. Viewing a lesson is a navigation record; a website Finish marker or locally saved practice is not a Canvas submission, grade, or proof of mastery.

One capstone, five cumulative milestones

Use an approved partner problem or the complete fictional Cedar Desk case. Keep one project record and submit five dated snapshots. A usable document-based AI-assisted service can meet the goal. Collect an observed user task, revise, retest, calculate value and capacity, shape a truthful offer or adoption case, and prepare a demonstration and operating handoff.

Communication and feedback

Use the course discussion for general questions and constructive peer exchange. Use Canvas Inbox for private assessment, access, or partner matters. Use the integrated course AI TA for explanations and hints. It uses public course content and cannot inspect Canvas submissions, grades, or saved work. Instructor assessment, comments, and returned rubric feedback are in Canvas.

Materials and tools

Use Canvas, a browser, an ordinary document or presentation tool, and a spreadsheet or calculator. An approved AI assistant may be used; no specific vendor or paid subscription is required. Supplied outputs and the classroom case provide an accessible alternative. Workbook and practice exports can support your evidence record; submit the required work in Canvas.

Learning Outcomes

  • Identify a real-world need, its stakeholders, and measurable success criteria.
  • Use a ready-made AI assistant or critically evaluate supplied AI outputs to improve business work while checking accuracy and usefulness.
  • Build and deliver a usable AI-assisted product, service, or testable prototype through existing tools, and document its pilot evaluation.
  • Compare AI and non-AI alternatives using customer evidence, costs, benefits, risks, and feasibility.
  • Interpret tables, figures, rates, descriptive statistics, uncertainty, and business costs without programming.
  • Plan a small pilot with human accountability, adoption support, evaluation measures, and stop conditions.
  • Plan customer acquisition, pricing, selling, onboarding, and delivery, then communicate an evidence-based recommendation and handoff to a partner or classroom client.

Late Work

Assignments are accepted up to 3 days late with a 10% penalty per day. After 3 days, no credit is given without prior arrangement. Contact the instructor before the deadline if you need an extension.

AI Use and Evidence

Use fictional or public non-sensitive information. Label case assumptions, simulated feedback, and AI-generated material. Verify important claims with credible sources. AI-generated customer personas and simulated interviews are practice material, not evidence of real customer demand. If access to an AI tool is unavailable, use the supplied sample output or complete the same analysis manually.

  • Disclose the AI tool used, its purpose, one representative prompt, and how you checked and changed its output.
  • You are responsible for every claim, calculation, citation, and recommendation in your submission.
  • Do not present fictional or simulated interviews, customers, performance, or financial results as real. Do not fabricate citations.
  • Grading rewards business judgment, evidence, and clear communication; polished AI output alone is insufficient.

Academic Integrity

Submit your own analysis, identify collaborators where applicable, and cite sources and AI assistance. Do not copy another student’s work or present invented evidence as real. See the UVU Academic Integrity Policy.
