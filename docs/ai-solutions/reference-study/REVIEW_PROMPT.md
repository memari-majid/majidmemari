# Instructions for an independent AI reviewer

Copy the task below into another AI with access to the repository and, if available, the live course. The reference website is optional: this study is sufficient to understand the design patterns. Do not pretend to have inspected online behavior if you only read these notes.

---

Review **Building AI Solutions by Majid Memari** for instructional clarity, visual explanation, interaction quality, numerical honesty, and capstone transfer. Recommend improvements grounded in evidence. Begin with the current [course review brief](../review-brief.md) and [repository map](../source-map.md). Inspect the learner experience before reading earlier judgments. Then use this [reference-study index](README.md) and [build handbook](BUILD_HANDBOOK.md) as a design benchmark.

### Owner requirements

- Business learners from Finance, Accounting, Marketing, MBA/Management, MIS and Operations can use the same path.
- Students do not program. They use numbers, tables, figures, AI tools and business judgment.
- The capstone goes from a real need through an implemented and evaluated solution, offer/adoption case, deployment, users/sales and operational handoff.
- Partners and available resources can change each offering; there must be a complete fictional fallback.
- Keep one clear twelve-unit path and Learn → Try → Build → Finish. Embed simulations in the lessons.
- Keep Majid Memari as the sole instructor/owner. Public branding is independent of a university or course number.
- Preserve a consistent visual theme across the personal website, course and assistants.
- Course TA and website chatbot are separate. Required learning cannot depend on either assistant working.
- On the wider website, retain LinkedIn but do not publish an email address; communication and inquiries go through the website chatbot.

### Review method

1. Identify the exact source commit and URL/date inspected. Record whether each finding comes from source, local build, preview, production, or learner observation.
2. Walk the course as a new business learner: find Start, state the task, make a decision, recover from a wrong choice, create evidence, save/resume and reach Finish. Record confusion and unnecessary branches.
3. For every simulation, identify its misconception, learner action, data, linked views, numerical consequence, feedback and capstone transfer. A slider or animation without one of these is not automatically useful.
4. Trace one small example through chart, table, caption and cost. Verify shared cohort, units, denominator, rounding and baseline. Try all/none selected, zero denominators, ties, exact budget limits and unsupported inputs where applicable.
5. Test a correct and an incorrect response; inspect whether feedback explains the mechanism and whether revision is possible. Test state retention, resets and interrupted animations.
6. Inspect keyboard operation, visible focus, small-screen layout, zoom, reduced motion, chart alternatives and non-color meaning. Distinguish spot checks from a full accessibility audit.
7. Compare the design with the reference patterns only after the independent walkthrough. Identify a concrete opportunity to improve learning; do not recommend copying its words, data, diagrams, branding or entire statistics syllabus.
8. Inspect whether the course TA can retrieve the public explanation and cite the current lesson. Do not assume it can calculate live activity state merely because the page has a chart. Keep private notes and partner data out of the retrieval corpus.
9. Review whether each unit contributes usable capstone evidence and whether milestones evaluate demonstrated competence rather than completing clicks.
10. State which recommendations need a representative-learner pilot rather than more engineering.

### Scoring rubric

Use 0–3 per criterion: **0 absent/broken; 1 present but weak; 2 useful with a specific gap; 3 strong in the evidence inspected**. Use **not assessed** instead of guessing. Do not turn a total score into a learning-effectiveness claim.

| Criterion | Evidence to seek |
|---|---|
| Orientation | Learner knows where to start, what success means and where to go next |
| Decision relevance | Activity changes a real business decision or operating policy |
| Mechanism clarity | A visible transformation explains why the result happens |
| Linked representations | The same records and calculations drive chart, table and explanation |
| Meaningful visuals | Axes, units, marks, color roles and scales are explained |
| Feedback | Wrong and correct choices both produce useful reasoning |
| Revision | Learner can explore, revise and distinguish attempts |
| Statistical honesty | Uncertainty, synthetic assumptions and causal limits are explicit |
| Economic honesty | Costs, capacity, cash, estimates and observed outcomes are distinct |
| Accessibility | Keyboard, text/table alternatives, focus and motion behavior are usable |
| Mobile readability | Reading flow and essential controls remain usable at narrow widths |
| Persistence | Saved progress and resets behave as stated |
| Capstone transfer | Simulation output becomes evidence for the learner's project |
| Partner adaptability | The method survives a different partner and constrained resources |
| Assistant integration | Course TA is grounded, optional and visually integrated |
| Maintainability | A new AI can trace source ownership, build, tests and live deployment |

### Required output

Return:

1. A short assessment of readiness and the most consequential gap.
2. An evidence table: finding, severity, exact file/route/section, observed behavior, why it matters, proposed change, and a concrete verification step.
3. A prioritized set of improvements: **fix before teaching**, **next iteration**, and **optional enrichment**. Prefer a few high-value changes over adding more navigation or controls.
4. One worked redesign of an existing activity: current misconception → proposed interaction → linked numerical evidence → feedback → capstone transfer. Use original data and wording.
5. Coverage and limitations, including untested devices/states and claims requiring external subject review or a learner study.

A finding must be actionable and supported. Do not invent missing features without searching the current source and UI. Do not treat historical offering files or archived plans as the active course. Do not include private records, secrets or account screenshots. This review task does not authorize publishing, account changes, student messages, grading changes or deleting files.
