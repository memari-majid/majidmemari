# Building AI Solutions — asynchronous delivery plan

**Owner and sole instructor:** Majid Memari  
**Date:** November 11, 2026  
**Status:** Planning specification before implementation. This document does not establish that the live course is ready for asynchronous teaching.

**Framing:** Practical AI problem-solving for everyone. Students from any discipline can participate with no prerequisites. Do not assume programming, prior AI or business knowledge, experience with a particular tool, or paid AI access. Explain necessary vocabulary, calculations, and tool steps; retain a supplied-output route.

The stable design remains **12 ordered units, one continuing capstone, five milestones, and Learn → Try → Build → Finish**. This plan adds the support needed to complete that path without attending a live class. Business cases, economics, adoption, and real-world implementation remain useful learning material.

## 2. One learner routine

The homepage presents a single **Start with Unit 1** action, the twelve-unit path, and five milestone positions. It explains where to find Grades, feedback, course questions, private help, accessibility support, and the final completion checklist. Orientation belongs here and at the beginning of Unit 1; it is not a competing pathway or thirteenth unit.

Each module uses:

1. **Lesson and reading:** outcomes, estimated time, original core explanation, worked example/table, embedded Try activity, Build instruction, and Finish recap. A readable text version is available at the point of use.
2. **Project exchange where useful:** only in Units 3, 7, and 12, before the quiz, with the fallback described below. It is not a separate scored submission.
3. **Practice quiz and project update:** three short choice questions with explanatory feedback plus one zero-point essay/evidence field. In exchange units, that field also records the learner's Canvas contribution/feedback and change, or their critique of the provided fictional sample. This replaces the earlier unit check-in; it does not add a second response paper.
4. **Milestone when due:** after Units 3, 5, 7, 10, and 12. Unit 12 also contains the individual case exam within Milestone 5's existing grade allocation.

Use Canvas Next/Previous and keep simulations and the TA inside the lesson. Do not create another required simulation destination. No live attendance, public launch, payment, paid subscription, or partner access is required. Milestone work can use a document kit or supervised service that another person can operate.

Module checks record viewing or submission, not correct understanding. No hard prerequisite or minimum-score locks are needed. A learner who misses a practice question receives a specific explanation and can retry. Publish a suggested pace rather than invent offering dates; any actual deadlines belong to the offering calendar.

## 3. Lessons, readings, and equivalent access

For each unit, provide the original course explanation and required tables in a native Canvas reading/transcript page or an accessible text section in the lesson page. This is the authored course content, not copied publisher chapters. Place the relevant interactive figure or simulation beside its explanation. A text/table worked example must teach the same decision without JavaScript, animation, an AI account, or access to the hosted frame.

The unit header states its learning outcomes, what to produce, required reading, optional further reading, and estimated effort. Keep one focused external reading and its purpose where useful, but ensure the original core explanation supports the assessed task if that link fails. Verify link accessibility before launch; do not promise unrestricted reuse of linked publisher material. Optional books must not become a hidden purchase requirement.

Provide the Cedar Desk source card, observation/test templates, project record, economics table, and handoff checklist as accessible native text or downloadable editable files. A learner must be able to find them from the unit that uses them. Provide a complete filled example with supplied facts clearly labeled, plus at least one flawed example that demonstrates how to use the rubric.

The current guided-work estimate is 65–115 minutes per unit plus additional project work. This plan's quizzes replace the current Finish/check-in response, and discussions replace some project critique; they must not silently expand workload. Show provisional per-item time estimates and revise them after a learner pilot. Do not call them measured completion times or academic-credit equivalence.

## 4. Native practice quizzes

Create **12 practice quizzes**, each with three short choice items and one zero-point project-update essay. One choice item can adapt the canonical Finish question; two should be original transfer or misconception checks that have actually been taught. This creates **36 choice items**, not a new technical survey.

| Unit | Main practice and transfer target |
|---|---|
| 1 | AI draft versus evidence, source checking, human authority, usable task |
| 2 | Neutral discovery, contrary evidence, mean/median/total, sample denominators |
| 3 | Consistent alternatives, full-process effort, quality boundaries, feasible scope |
| 4 | Workflow roles, source validity, missing facts, exception and authorization |
| 5 | Usable artifact versus mock-up, varied bench cases, meaningful revision/retest |
| 6 | Fair comparison, complete effort and quality, percentage reduction and limits |
| 7 | Actual observation versus supplied example, denominator/cohort, retest and inference |
| 8 | Capacity versus cash, costs and periods, adoption sensitivity and named threshold |
| 9 | Truthful offer, scope, contribution, excluded costs, whole-package capacity |
| 10 | Channel/funnel denominators, actual paying customer, acquisition cost, adoption next step |
| 11 | Current approved sources, owner review, queue/capacity, support and stopping rules |
| 12 | Evidence-backed recommendation, honest chart, changed assumption, handoff and limits |

Every choice item includes all required facts, one defensible answer, plausible misconception-based alternatives, and feedback explaining the reasoning for each answer. Numerical feedback shows units, denominator, calculation, and the resulting decision. Link or name the relevant lesson section. Avoid trick wording, unsupported claims, or distractors that are merely absurd.

The essay uses the canonical project prompt in a concise format: what changed, the evidence/source, what remains uncertain, and the next action. It is formative and does not create another paper. Automatic choice feedback is immediate; essay comments are instructor feedback and may remain pending. Do not claim the essay is automatically checked or that receiving a submission proves mastery.

For the authorized receiving-course implementation, use native graded quizzes with three one-point choice items and a zero-point evidence essay in the **zero-weight formative group**. The technical quiz type enables native submission and feedback; its raw practice score contributes **0%** to the course grade. Set final-grade omission where supported and verify the effective zero contribution. Label that distinction in student instructions. Allow retries and use submission, not a particular practice score or manual essay grade, for module progress. Verify the actual engine's essay, feedback, retry, completion, and student-visible score behavior after import.

Instructure distinguishes Practice Quiz from Graded Quiz in its [official quiz-type guide](https://community.instructure.com/en/kb/articles/660994-unknown). “Practice” in this plan describes the instructional purpose; the selected native Graded Quiz implementation must remain explicitly formative and contribute zero course weight. Recheck quiz-engine availability when moving to another institution.

## 5. Purposeful asynchronous discussions

Keep **Course questions and project exchange** as an optional, ungraded help forum, linked from the homepage throughout the course. Add only three structured formative exchanges:

| Unit | Prompt and contribution to the same project |
|---|---|
| 3 | Present the problem, one contrary observation, and bounded scope. Give a peer one evidence question and one scope improvement; revise or defend the charter. |
| 7 | Present a small actual-versus-supplied evidence table, defect, revision/retest, and one limitation. Give a peer one denominator/comparison check and one next-test suggestion. |
| 12 | Present the demonstration/decision brief, one key figure, and the requested next step. Give a peer one supported strength and one consequential challenge; record the final revision. |

Each exchange is ungraded and requires one concise post plus constructive feedback on a peer or a provided fictional sample. Seed the sample before opening the module. Learners do not have to wait for a peer, join a synchronous meeting, or expose private partner/customer information. If module contribution tracking is used, explain that a contribution check records participation, not the quality or number of replies; instructions and review establish quality. No score weight is added.

Where an accessible individual alternative is needed, the learner critiques the same provided sample in the quiz's project-update field. Configure the corresponding completion path honestly rather than require a forum post the learner cannot make. General questions belong in the forum; private grades, accommodations, or individual circumstances use Canvas Inbox. The TA explains public course content and cannot read Canvas submissions or make assessment decisions.

## 6. Milestones and grade integrity

Keep the existing five weighted groups: **15%, 20%, 25%, 15%, 25%**. Milestones 1–4 retain their 100-point assignments, canonical criteria, evidence checklist, and placement. Each assignment states accepted file/text/URL formats, reviewer access instructions, what belongs in the submission, estimated effort, rubric, feedback expectation, and revision route. A snapshot updates one project; it does not repeat all earlier background.

For Milestone 5, separate the already reserved individual component into a native **Individual case exam** assignment. Do not add a sixth weighted group or a new grade percentage.

| Item in the existing Milestone 5 group | Points | Share of whole course |
|---|---:|---:|
| Demonstration and evidence-backed recommendation | 25 | 6.25% |
| Operating handoff and continued use | 20 | 5% |
| Executive communication | 15 | 3.75% |
| Individual case exam — quantitative understanding | 20 | 5% |
| Individual case exam — design judgment and ownership | 20 | 5% |
| **Milestone 5 group total** | **100** | **25%** |

The demonstration/handoff assignment therefore has **60 points** and the individual exam **40 points** in the same 25% group. The combined assessment preserves the canonical five criteria and their allocations. Earlier individual evidence near Milestone 3 is formative preparation and supporting evidence, not a second numerical grade. Do not retain a 100-point Milestone 5 assignment beside the new 40-point exam; that would silently alter the intended allocation.

The exam is an asynchronous, open-materials, individually authored case response, using a new original scenario with four prompts: two numerical interpretations and two consequential design/evidence decisions. It includes sufficient case facts and calculations that can be performed with a calculator. At least one prompt connects a changed condition to the learner's own contribution. The grading guide explicitly covers acceptable alternative judgments, units and denominators, evidence limits, human responsibility, and the reason for the next action.

The instructor-only model reasoning and partial-credit guide is stored at the ignored path `private/canvas/instructor-exam-guide.md` under the canonical course. Exclude it from the student cartridge, generated public course, hosting documentation mirrors, TA corpus, and public review bundles. Student-facing exam fields contain the case, prompts, checklist, and rubric only.

Publish the permitted AI-use rule before the exam. Recommended for this applied course: course materials, a calculator, and disclosed AI assistance are allowed, while the learner must author and defend the reasoning, identify sources, and record what was checked or corrected. This assesses documented individual judgment; do not describe it as proctored proof of unaided performance. A full generated response or fabricated project evidence does not meet the criteria. Provide a written alternative to any presentation or recording requirement.

Use one initial submission plus a revision opportunity after feedback, with the final reviewed submission counting; announce the offering's actual arrangements. The instructor grades the individual exam using the two 20-point criteria. Do not represent it as automatically scored. No compulsory live oral exam is needed for the asynchronous baseline.

## 7. A complete self-paced support route

Before launch, publish realistic response and feedback windows. Planning defaults are two working days for course/private questions and five working days for milestones and the exam; the instructor must confirm or revise these for the actual offering. Explain how to find comments and rubrics, resubmit, interpret an incomplete item, and request help. While waiting for scope feedback, a learner can practice with Cedar Desk but cannot treat silence or TA advice as approval for consequential external work.

Partner access is optional. Cedar Desk supplies the complete source card, inputs, benchmark, and templates. It does not supply a learner's actual observation. For the required user-task evidence, students can arrange an asynchronous attempt with a classmate, colleague, friend, or assigned classroom reviewer. If no reviewer is available, the instructor arranges a bounded review and retest through Canvas. A provided worked example, self-review, or AI role-play cannot be relabeled as another person's observed use.

Estimate and resource this reviewer route before enrollment. For example, ten teams needing a 15-minute combined task/retest review require about 2.5 hours beyond ordinary teaching and assessment; thirty individual projects require about 7.5 hours. These are planning assumptions. Do not promise instant individual review from one instructor.

Provide recovery instructions for an unavailable embedded application, an unavailable TA, a broken reading link, a failed artifact link, and a missed feedback window. Original text, worked tables, supplied outputs, and Canvas submissions keep the core work possible. Browser-local activity state and TA conversation are not the submission record; the learner's Canvas response and attached evidence are.

Carry these into native lesson/transcript pages, quiz instructions, discussion prompts, assignments, exam, rubrics, and downloadable templates. ### Acceptance checks

- **Content completeness:** exactly 12 units on one path; each has outcomes, original required reading/transcript, worked example/table, purposeful practice, Build prompt, feedback, and next step. Five milestone placements and the Cedar Desk fallback are intact.
- **Question integrity:** 36 choice items have reviewed answers and misconception-specific feedback; all facts are present, calculations independently agree, no specialist prerequisite is hidden, and the 12 evidence fields align to the project prompts.
- **Assessment boundaries:** practice and discussions contribute no course percentage. Groups total 100%; Milestones 1–4 retain their weights; Milestone 5 is exactly 60+40 within 25%. Earlier individual checks are not scored twice. Legacy groups cannot contribute.
- **Native behavior:** test a wrong answer, correct answer, retry, zero-point essay, submission history, pending feedback state, instructor comment, milestone rubric grade, exam grade, and resubmission. Verify actual student-visible behavior rather than only XML fields.
- **Progress:** view a lesson, submit practice, contribute to a discussion or its approved alternative, and submit a milestone/exam; requirements update appropriately without score locks. Old check-ins and superseded assessments do not present another active path.
- **Asynchronous completion:** a test learner can identify what to do, produce, submit, revise, and finish without attending live or finding a peer currently online. A route to an actual reviewer attempt is available and accurately labeled.
- **Equivalent access:** test native reading without the frame/TA, keyboard, zoom, narrow screens, reduced motion, readable tables, figure text equivalents, accessible file formats, and print. Offer alternatives to audio/video submissions.
- **Theme:** inspect homepage, all 12 lesson wrappers, practice quiz, discussion, milestone, rubric instructions, exam, and transcript alongside the website for coherent typography, spacing, color meaning, and next-action wording.
- **Operational readiness:** dates or self-paced expectations, response windows, grading policy, reviewer arrangements, accessibility route, and final completion definition are published before students enroll. Receiving-instance content and course publication states are checked separately.
- **Evidence honesty:** record the source version, imported package, record counts, tests, and remaining limits. A successful build/import or AI review does not establish learning effectiveness. Pilot with learners from several disciplines, including AI novices, before calling the course validated.

This plan is satisfied only when the authored materials, native assessment behavior, feedback route, and complete learner path are implemented and observed. Keep the course described as an asynchronous design under development until those checks pass.
